\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A builder of agent systems repeatedly faces the same decision: given a
verifiable task, a deployment budget, and a menu ranging from inexpensive
specialists to frontier generalists, which system should be run? The choice
extends beyond the backbone model to the solving strategy, the information
available at deployment, and the allocation of compute across repeated
attempts. The relevant objective is therefore to identify the configuration
that reaches a verified solution most efficiently.

The usual answer is benchmark racing: fix the workflow, compare aggregate
scores, and select the winner. This ranks systems but does not explain the
ranking. A system may win because each step is cheaper, because it needs fewer
steps on the relevant instances, because an observable signal identifies the
appropriate strategy, or because its allocation policy wastes less compute.
Those mechanisms imply different interventions, yet an aggregate score combines
them into one number. Related questions arise in algorithm portfolios,
per-instance selection, and model routing
\citep{gomes-selman2001,xu_hutter_hoos_leytonbrown_2008_satzilla,
chen2023frugalgpt,ong2024routellm}.

We study this problem for verifiable tasks, including code generation against
tests, formal proof search, and verifier-backed planning. Success is determined
by an external verifier, allowing performance to be measured by \emph{proper
time}: the wall-clock time, tokens, or cost consumed before the first verified
solution. This operationalizes the time-to-solution perspective of Achille and
Soatto~\citep{achille2026} for finite-budget model selection.

We represent task heterogeneity through solver-relevant modes, each associated
with a specialized solving strategy. The worker model determines the cost and
competence of executing these strategies. Before allocating compute, a router
observes a signal $Z$, such as task metadata, demonstrations, or partial
traces. This induces a posterior $\pi_Z$ over modes, while the router implements
an allocation $q_Z$ of the retry budget across strategies. Models and modes
therefore remain distinct objects.

Under a packed inverse-share law, the expected log proper-time gain satisfies
\[
    \Delta_{\log}
    =
    I(S;Z)
    -
    \mathbb E_Z\!\left[
        \mathrm{KL}(\pi_Z\|q_Z)
    \right].
\]
The first term is the speed potentially available from the signal; the second
is the amount lost through allocation mismatch. The identity also prescribes
the optimal allocation under the packed law: $q_Z=\pi_Z$.

When worker models differ, the decomposition becomes
\[
    \Delta_{M,\log}
    =
    \log\frac{\kappa(M_0)}{\kappa(M)}
    +
    \varphi(M_0,M)
    +
    G(M)
    -
    \varepsilon(M),
\]
separating per-step cost, mode-conditioned competence, information, and
allocation mismatch. It provides the quantities required to decide when a
larger model, cheaper retries, or signal-conditioned strategy allocation
minimizes verified time.

We evaluate the framework on HumanEval+ using three worker models, three
specialized strategies, and twenty attempts per model--strategy--task
configuration, for $29{,}520$ verified attempts. After selecting the router
and allocation rule on validation data, the resulting policy achieves a
$1.113\times$ solver-time speedup on a disjoint test set at fixed 7B worker
and unchanged verified success ($37/42$ tasks under both policies). This
comparison isolates allocation speedup and excludes router inference latency.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We formulate model selection for verifiable tasks as the joint choice
    of model, strategy, and retry allocation under a deployment budget.

    \item We prove that information-driven expected log-speedup equals signal
    information minus allocation mismatch under the packed law.

    \item We extend the identity to separate model cost, competence,
    information, and mismatch, obtaining model and retry crossover criteria.

    \item We introduce a cross-fitted measurement protocol and evaluate it on
    HumanEval+ using verified first-passage traces and held-out routing.
\end{enumerate}

\section{A Mathematical Framework for Agentic Tasks}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{Tasks, Solvers, and Agent Systems}

\begin{definition}[Verifiable task]
\label{def:task}
A \emph{verifiable task} is a tuple
\[
\cT^{(v)}=(\cX,\cY,V,\mu,\cB),
\]
where $\cX$ is the instance space, $\cY$ is the solution space,
$V:\cX\times\cY\to\{0,1\}$ is an external verification oracle that returns
$1$ exactly when a candidate satisfies the task's acceptance criterion,
$\mu$ is the deployment distribution over $\cX$, and
$\cB=(b_t,b_c,b_m)$ specifies time, cost, and memory budgets.
Success on instance $x$ means producing $y$ such that $V(x,y)=1$ within $\cB$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Agent system and induced solver]
\label{def:agent-system}
For a verifiable task $\cT^{(v)}$, an \emph{agent system} is specified by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-tuple}
d=(M,\mathcal H,\rho)\in\cD,
\end{equation}
where $M$ is the worker model, $\mathcal H$ is a finite set of solving
strategies or harnesses, and $\rho$ allocates the available attempt budget
across those strategies using deployment-side information.  These components
induce a stochastic solver $A_d$ that executes model--strategy attempts and
emits candidate artifacts until $V$ accepts one or the budget is exhausted.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Proper Time and Certified Speed}

Fix a resource clock $c$: wall-clock time, tokens, API cost, verifier calls, or
another declared scalar resource.

\begin{definition}[Proper time and certified proper time]
\label{def:proper-time}
For solver $A$ on $(x,r)$, define its \emph{operational proper time} by
\[
\begin{gathered}
\tau_A(x;r):=
\inf\{c\ge 0:V(x,Y_A(c;x,r))=1\},\\
\qquad \inf\varnothing:=\infty.
\end{gathered}
\]
\label{def:certified-time}
and its \emph{certified proper time} at confidence $1-\delta$ by
\[
\overline{T}_A(\delta)
:=
\inf\!\left\{c\ge 0:
\Prb_{X\sim\mu,r}[\tau_A(X;r)\le c]\ge 1-\delta
\right\}.
\]
The former is trajectory-level; the latter is its deployment quantile over
$X\sim\mu$ and $r$.  Its empirical estimate is the corresponding sample
quantile, with failures recorded as $\infty$.
\label{def:speedup}
Relative to baseline $A_0$, the \emph{certified log-speedup} is
$\Delta:=\log\overline T_{A_0}(\delta)-\log\overline T_A(\delta)$; positive
$\Delta$ means that $A$ is faster.
\end{definition}

The packed-family identities below concern an expected log-time surrogate
$\Dlog$.  Certified quantile speedup and operational first-passage speedup are
reported separately in the empirical analysis.

\subsection{The Packed Latent-Mode Family}
\label{subsec:packed-family-def}

\begin{definition}[Packed allocation policy]
\label{def:routing-policy}
Let $\cS$ be a finite mode set and let $Z$ take values in a signal space
$\mathcal Z$.  A \emph{packed allocation policy} is a map
$\rho:\mathcal Z\to\Delta(\cS)$, written $z\mapsto q_z$, whose coordinate
$q_z(s)$ is the fraction of the attempt budget assigned to the strategy
specialized for mode~$s$.  The signal $Z$ contains the information available
when the allocation is chosen.  A no-routing baseline uses a fixed allocation
$q_0$ independent of~$z$.
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Packed latent-mode family]
\label{def:packed-family}
\label{ass:packed-family}
Fix a verifiable task $\cT^{(v)}$.
Assume:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Latent modes.} There is a finite set of modes $\cS=\{1,\ldots,m\}$ and a latent variable $S \in \cS$ with prior $\pi$. A mode is a solver-relevant class of instances, defined independently of which model is deployed. An observable signal $Z \sim P(Z \mid S)$ represents admissible pre-deployment evidence or partial execution traces, with posterior $\pi_z(s) := \Prb[S = s \mid Z=z]$.
\item \textbf{One strategy per mode.} There is a strategy set
$\mathcal H=\{h_s:s\in\cS\}$ in which $h_s$ is specialized for mode~$s$ and
has packed internal scale $t_0(s)>0$.
\item \textbf{Allocation determines time.} A routing policy chooses a share vector $q \in \Delta(\cS)$.
Under hidden mode~$s$, allocating share $q(s)$ yields certified time
\[
\mathsf{T}_q(s) \;=\; c_{\mathrm{sim}} \cdot \frac{t_0(s)}{q(s)},
\]
where $c_{\mathrm{sim}}>0$ is the simulation overhead.
\item \textbf{Baseline and deployed schedulers.} The baseline chooses a fixed share vector $q_0 \in \Delta(\cS)$; the deployed system chooses a data-dependent map $z \mapsto q_z \in \Delta(\cS)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

All KL and logarithmic expressions below use the standing support convention
that every allocation puts positive mass on the posterior support.  For a
posterior $\pi_z$ over modes, define
$\Meff(z):=\exp(H(\pi_z))$.

Given the feasible designs $d=(M,\mathcal H,\rho)$, the builder minimizes
certified proper time over model, strategy set, and allocation policy.  The
following decomposition identifies the mechanisms determining this objective.

\section{Information-Routing and Model-Aware Decompositions}
\label{sec:decomposition}

\subsection{The Routing-Information Identity}
\label{subsec:ri-identity}

\begin{lemma}[Posterior-matched allocation]
\label{lem:conditional}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:packed-family}, for $P_Z$-almost every $z$ and every
$q\in\Delta(\cS)$ such that
$\operatorname{supp}(\pi_z)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(q)$,
\[
\E_{S\sim\pi_z}\!\left[
\log\frac{\mathsf T_q(S)}{c_{\mathrm{sim}}t_0(S)}
\right]
=H(\pi_z)+\KL(\pi_z\|q).
\]
The unique minimizer of $\E_{S\sim\pi_z}[\log\mathsf T_q(S)]$ over admissible
allocations is $q=\pi_z$.  The corresponding excess packed log-time is
$\KL(\pi_z\|q)$.
\end{lemma}

\par\noindent\emph{Proof.} See Appendix~\ref{app:proof:lem:conditional}.\par


\begin{theorem}[Routing-Information Identity]
\label{thm:ri-identity}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:packed-family}, with a prior-matched baseline $q_0 = \pi$,
\boxeddisplay{\Dlog = G - \varepsilon}
where
\begin{align}
\makebox[4.4em][l]{$\Dlog$}
&:= \E\!\left[\log \mathsf{T}_\pi(S)-\log \mathsf{T}_{q_Z}(S)\right], \nonumber\\
\makebox[4.4em][l]{$G$}
&:= \I_\pi(S : Z)=H(\pi)-\E_Z[H(\pi_Z)], \label{eq:G-def} \\
\makebox[4.4em][l]{$\varepsilon$}
&:= \E_Z\!\left[\KL(\pi_Z \| q_Z)\right]. \label{eq:eps-def}
\end{align}
The term $G$ is the mutual information between the latent mode and the feedback---the \emph{information generation quality}.
The term $\varepsilon$ is the expected KL divergence of the routing policy from the posterior---the \emph{routing mismatch}.
Equivalently: $\Dlog \le G$, with equality if and only if $q_Z = \pi_Z$ almost surely.
One nat of routing mismatch costs exactly one nat of expected packed
log-speedup.
\end{theorem}

\par\noindent\emph{Proof.} See Appendix~\ref{app:proof:thm:ri-identity}.\par


For a non-prior-matched baseline $q_0 \ne \pi$, the identity becomes $\Dlog = G + \KL(\pi \| q_0) - \varepsilon$, adding a baseline suboptimality bonus.

\subsection{The Model-Aware Decomposition}
\label{subsec:four-term}

\begin{assumption}[Model-dependent packed family]
\label{ass:model-packed-family}
Fix a verifiable task and a shared latent mode variable $S \sim \pi$ over $\cS=\{1,\ldots,m\}$.
For each model $M$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The per-step cost satisfies $\kappa(M)>0$.
\item The within-mode packed scale satisfies $t_0(M,s)>0$, so that packed time
under mode~$s$ with allocation share $q(s)$ is
\[
\mathsf{T}_q^{(M)}(s) = \kappa(M) \cdot c_{\mathrm{sim}} \cdot \frac{t_0(M,s)}{q(s)}.
\]
\item The feedback signal is $Z_M \sim P_M(Z \mid S)$, with posterior $\pi_z^M(s) := \Prb[S = s \mid Z_M = z]$ for a realization $z$.
\item The routing policy is $q_z^M \in \Delta(\cS)$ for each realized signal
$z$, with
$\operatorname{supp}(\pi_z^M)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(q_z^M)$ almost
surely.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Model-Aware Decomposition]
\label{thm:four-term}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:model-packed-family}, comparing a prior-matched baseline using model $M_0$ against a deployed system using model~$M$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:four-term}
\resizebox{0.88\columnwidth}{!}{$\boxed{\displaystyle
\DlogM
=\log\frac{\kappa(M_0)}{\kappa(M)}
+\varphi(M_0,M)+G(M)-\varepsilon(M)}$}
\end{equation}
where the four terms are defined by
\begin{align}
\makebox[6.0em][l]{$\DlogM$}
&:=\E\!\left[
\log \mathsf{T}_\pi^{(M_0)}(S)
-\log \mathsf{T}_{q_Z}^{(M)}(S)
\right], \nonumber\\
\makebox[6.0em][l]{$C_\kappa(M_0,M)$}
&:=\log\frac{\kappa(M_0)}{\kappa(M)}, \label{eq:kappa-term} \\
\makebox[6.0em][l]{$\varphi(M_0,M)$}
&:=\E_\pi\!\left[\log\frac{t_0(M_0,S)}{t_0(M,S)}\right], \label{eq:phi-term} \\
\makebox[6.0em][l]{$G(M)$}
&:=\I_\pi(S : Z_M), \label{eq:G-term} \\
\makebox[6.0em][l]{$\varepsilon(M)$}
&:=\E_{Z_M}\!\left[\KL(\pi_Z^M \| q_Z^M)\right]. \label{eq:eps-term}
\end{align}
Here $C_\kappa$, $\varphi$, $G$, and $\varepsilon$ denote per-step cost,
within-mode competence, information quality, and routing mismatch, respectively.
\end{theorem}

\par\noindent\emph{Proof.} See Appendix~\ref{app:proof:thm:four-term}.\par

Appendix Proposition~\ref{prop:mode-dependent-cost} gives the more general identity in which per-step cost may also vary by mode and the baseline allocation need not equal~$\pi$.


Benchmark racing reports only an aggregate comparison. The decomposition reports why the expected log-time gain has its value.

\subsection{Scope and Metric Guardrails}
\label{subsec:guardrails}

The preceding identities hold exactly under the packed latent-mode assumptions
and concern expected packed log-time.  In particular,
\[
\E[\log T_0-\log T_1]\neq \log\frac{\E[T_0]}{\E[T_1]}
\quad\text{in general.}
\]
If the packed log-time law has a uniform additive residual
$|r_M|\le\eta$, Proposition~\ref{prop:approximation-guarantee} adds a single
term $R$ satisfying $|R|\le2\eta$.


\section{Consequences for Model Choice and System Design}
\label{sec:consequences}

\begin{corollary}[Model-selection crossover]
\label{cor:model-selection}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:model-packed-family}, model $M_1$ achieves lower
expected packed log-time than model $M_2$ whenever
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:crossover}
\begin{aligned}
&
\log\frac{\kappa(M_2)}{\kappa(M_1)}
+\varphi(M_0,M_1)-\varphi(M_0,M_2)
\\[-1pt]
&\qquad>G(M_2)-G(M_1)
+\varepsilon(M_1)-\varepsilon(M_2).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\par\noindent\emph{Proof.} See Appendix~\ref{app:proof:cor:model-selection}.\par

\begin{theorem}[Single-mode retry crossover]
\label{thm:retry-crossover}
Fix one homogeneous mode and a common one-attempt horizon.  Let a stronger
system have success probability $p_h$ and per-attempt cost $\kappa_h$, and let
a cheaper system have $0<p_\ell<p_h<1$ and
$0<\kappa_\ell<\kappa_h$.  Assume retries of the cheaper system are
conditionally independent and identically distributed.  The smallest retry
depth matching the stronger system's one-attempt reliability is
\[
D_{\mathrm{cross}}
=
\left\lceil
\frac{\log(1-p_h)}{\log(1-p_\ell)}
\right\rceil.
\]
Executing all $D_{\mathrm{cross}}$ attempts is cheaper if and only if
$D_{\mathrm{cross}}\kappa_\ell<\kappa_h$.  With stopping at first success,
the expected cost is
\[
\kappa_\ell
\frac{1-(1-p_\ell)^{D_{\mathrm{cross}}}}{p_\ell}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\par\noindent\emph{Proof.} See Appendix~\ref{app:proof:retry-crossover}.\par

The inner routing problem of the packed family is the same log-optimal allocation problem that appears in Kelly betting~\cite{kelly1956}, universal portfolio theory~\cite{cover-thomas2006}, and algorithm-portfolio scheduling~\cite{gomes-selman2001}. The optimal allocation is $q^*(\cdot \mid Z)=\pi_Z(\cdot)$; the mismatch $\varepsilon$ is the expected log-loss from suboptimal allocation.

\section{Application and Measurement}
\label{sec:application}
\label{sec:measurement}

\subsection{Experimental Design}

We evaluate the framework on all 164 HumanEval+ tasks.  Before any model run,
we partition tasks into three equally sized modes using reference-solution
length, with cutoffs at 35 and 58 tokens.  The mode labels are used to fit and
evaluate posteriors but are not supplied as deployment labels.  We pair the
modes with three strategy-specific harnesses: a direct implementation prompt,
a structured specification-and-invariants prompt, and a robust decomposition
and edge-case prompt.

We execute Qwen2.5-Coder 1.5B, 7B, and 32B with each strategy for twenty
independent sampling attempts per task.  This gives
$164\times3\times3\times20=29{,}520$ verifier-scored attempts.  Every record
contains model, strategy, task mode, generated tokens, wall-clock time, and
HumanEval+ verification.  Models and modes are therefore distinct: the model
controls attempt cost and competence, while the allocation $q_Z$ distributes a
twenty-retry budget among strategies.

The router observes task metadata and either 0, 5, or 20 cross-fitted examples.
Its calibrated output estimates $\pi_Z$; an allocation head converts this
posterior into integer strategy counts summing to twenty.  The fixed baseline
is $q_0=(7,6,7)/20$.  Five-fold predictions estimate the decomposition terms.
A separate 81/41/42 train/validation/test split is used once to select the
router configuration and evaluate operational allocation speedup.

$0.661$ in seconds and $0.707$ in tokens, compared with the ideal packed value
of~$1$.  We therefore report the exact packed accounting together with its
observed closure residual rather than identifying the packed law with the

\subsection{Estimators and Paired Validation}

For calibrated posteriors $\hat\pi_{Z_i}^M$ and realized allocations
$\hat q_i^M$, we estimate
\[
\hat G=H(\hat\pi)-\frac1N\sum_iH(\hat\pi_{Z_i}^M),
\qquad
\hat\varepsilon=\frac1N\sum_i
\KL(\hat\pi_{Z_i}^M\|\hat q_i^M),
\]
and estimate $\varphi$ from mode-conditioned packed scales.  Temperature
calibration and every allocation-head fit use only training or validation
records.  Task bootstrap preserves the paired comparison between baseline and
routed allocations.

\begin{proposition}[Paired allocation-gain estimator]
\label{prop:paired-unbiased}
Let $X_1,\ldots,X_N$ be i.i.d. deployment instances.  For condition
$k\in\{0,j\}$, let a fixed policy select action
$a_i^k=\rho_k(Z_k(X_i))$, where an action may be a retry allocation.  Let
$\ell(a,x;\xi)$ be an integrable deployment loss, and let
$\widehat m_j(x)$ be an unbiased estimate of the incremental signal cost
$m_j(x)$ in the same units as~$\ell$.  If each of $Q$ repeated runs has the
corresponding deployment distribution conditional on $(X_i,a_i^k)$, then
\begin{align*}
\overline\ell_i^k
&:=\frac1Q\sum_{q=1}^Q\ell(a_i^k,X_i;\xi_{i,q}^k),\\
\widehat\Delta_R(j)
&:=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N
\left[\overline\ell_i^0-\overline\ell_i^j-\widehat m_j(X_i)\right]
\end{align*}
is unbiased for the expected deployment gain net of signal cost.
\end{proposition}

\par\noindent\emph{Proof.} See Appendix~\ref{app:proof:paired-estimator}.\par

\section{Related Work and Positioning}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Time and computational information.}
Achille and Soatto make time-to-solution central for verifiable tasks and
connect reusable information to speed~\cite{achille2026}.  Earlier
foundations include universal and resource-bounded search
\cite{solomonoff1964,levin1973,livitanyi2008,levin_1984_randomness_conservation,
schmidhuber_2002_speed_prior}.  We adopt the time-centric objective and study a
different question: how the deployment-time difference between two agent
designs separates into cost, competence, information, and allocation mismatch.

\paragraph{Selection, routing, and test-time compute.}
Algorithm portfolios and SATzilla select algorithms per instance rather than
committing to one global solver~\cite{gomes-selman2001,
xu_hutter_hoos_leytonbrown_2008_satzilla}.  FrugalGPT and RouteLLM bring related
cost--quality routing objectives to language models
\cite{chen2023frugalgpt,ong2024routellm}.  Adaptive test-time compute further
shows that the useful amount of inference effort depends on the prompt and the
policy used to spend it~\cite{snell2024testtime}.  These works motivate the
decision variables in our framework.  Our policy-agnostic identities expose the
terms traded by any allocation policy under the packed-family assumptions.

\paragraph{Sequential and modular computation.}
The retry analysis is related to anytime algorithms, metareasoning, and
sequential inspection~\cite{zilberstein_1996_anytime_systems,
hansen_2001_monitoring_anytime,russell_wefald_1991_dotherightthing,weitzman1979,
wald1947,chernoff1959}.  Mixture-of-experts and conditional computation make
routing quality an architectural concern~\cite{jordan_jacobs_1994_hme,
shazeer_2017_moe,fedus_zoph_shazeer_2022_switch}.  Our setting differs by
allocating an explicit retry budget among complete solving strategies and
measuring time to external verification.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The framework centers on externally verifiable packed latent modes with
$\mathsf{T}_q(s)\propto t_0(s)/q(s)$.  Uniform $\eta$-approximate
log-linearity gives residual at most $2\eta$.  HumanEval+ uses saved-trace
replay; prospective paired deployment additionally charges fresh
routing-information acquisition.

Open questions include dynamic mode discovery, correlated strategies,
end-to-end router-cost accounting, and direct tests of the inverse-share law
beyond the evaluated retry horizon.

The framework gives a decomposable objective, model-selection crossovers, and a
measurement protocol, organizing agent-design decisions around named,
estimable channels.

\section{Proofs for the Main Text}
\label{app:main-proofs}

\subsection{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:conditional}: Posterior-matched allocation}
\label{app:proof:lem:conditional}

\begin{proof}
By the inverse-share law,
$\log(\mathsf T_q(s)/(c_{\mathrm{sim}}t_0(s)))=-\log q(s)$.  Hence
\[
\E_{S\sim\pi_z}\!\left[
\log\frac{\mathsf T_q(S)}{c_{\mathrm{sim}}t_0(S)}
\right]
=\sum_s\pi_z(s)\log\frac{1}{q(s)}
=H(\pi_z)+\KL(\pi_z\|q),
\]
where the support condition makes every displayed term finite.  The intrinsic
term $\E_{S\sim\pi_z}[\log(c_{\mathrm{sim}}t_0(S))]$ is independent of~$q$.
Finally, $\KL(\pi_z\|q)\ge0$, with equality if and only if $q=\pi_z$.
This proves both uniqueness and the excess-loss identity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:ri-identity}: Routing-Information Identity}
\label{app:proof:thm:ri-identity}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:conditional} to the baseline allocation $q_0 = \pi$:
\[
\E\!\left[\log \mathsf{T}_\pi(S)\right]
= \E_\pi[\log(c_{\mathrm{sim}}\, t_0(S))] + H(\pi).
\]
Apply it to the deployed allocation $q_Z$:
\[
\E\!\left[\log \mathsf{T}_{q_Z}(S)\right]
= \E_\pi[\log(c_{\mathrm{sim}}\, t_0(S))] + \E_Z[H(\pi_Z)] + \E_Z[\KL(\pi_Z \| q_Z)].
\]
Subtract:
\[
\Dlog = H(\pi) - \E_Z[H(\pi_Z)] - \E_Z[\KL(\pi_Z \| q_Z)]
= G - \varepsilon.
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:four-term}: Model-Aware Decomposition}
\label{app:proof:thm:four-term}

\begin{proof}
Write out the log-time:
\[
\log \mathsf{T}_q^{(M)}(s) = \log \kappa(M) + \log c_{\mathrm{sim}} + \log t_0(M,s) - \log q(s).
\]
For the prior-matched baseline with model $M_0$:
\[
\E\!\left[\log \mathsf{T}_\pi^{(M_0)}(S)\right]
= \log\kappa(M_0) + \log c_{\mathrm{sim}} + \E_\pi[\log t_0(M_0,S)] + H(\pi).
\]
For the deployed system with model $M$:
\begin{align*}
\E\!\left[\log \mathsf{T}_{q_Z}^{(M)}(S)\right]
&= \log\kappa(M) + \log c_{\mathrm{sim}} + \E_\pi[\log t_0(M,S)] \\
&\quad + \E_{Z_M}[H(\pi_Z^M)] + \E_{Z_M}[\KL(\pi_Z^M \| q_Z^M)],
\end{align*}
where the last two terms arise from the cross-entropy identity exactly as in Theorem~\ref{thm:ri-identity}.
Subtracting and grouping:
\begin{align*}
\DlogM &= \log\frac{\kappa(M_0)}{\kappa(M)}
+ \E_\pi\!\left[\log\frac{t_0(M_0,S)}{t_0(M,S)}\right]
+ \bigl(H(\pi) - \E_{Z_M}[H(\pi_Z^M)]\bigr)
- \E_{Z_M}[\KL(\pi_Z^M \| q_Z^M)] \\[4pt]
&= \log\frac{\kappa(M_0)}{\kappa(M)} + \varphi(M_0,M) + G(M) - \varepsilon(M).
\end{align*}
\end{proof}

\subsection{Mode-dependent cost extension}

\begin{proposition}[Mode-dependent cost extension]
\label{prop:mode-dependent-cost}
Suppose $\kappa(M,s)>0$ may depend on both model and mode, and
\[
\mathsf{T}_q^{(M)}(s,z)
=c_{\mathrm{sim}}\,\kappa(M,s)\frac{t_0(M,s)}{q_z(s)}.
\]
For a baseline $(M_0,q_0)$ and a routed system $(M,q_Z)$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\E\!\left[\log\mathsf{T}_{q_0}^{(M_0)}(S)-\log\mathsf{T}_{q_Z}^{(M)}(S,Z)\right]\\
&\quad=
\E_{S\sim\pi}\!\left[\log\frac{\kappa(M_0,S)}{\kappa(M,S)}\right]
+\E_{S\sim\pi}\!\left[\log\frac{t_0(M_0,S)}{t_0(M,S)}\right]
+\I_\pi(S:Z)+\KL(\pi\|q_0)-\E_Z\KL(\pi_Z\|q_Z).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply the conditional cross-entropy identity to the allocation term. The tower property gives
$\E_Z\E_{S\sim\pi_Z}[\log\kappa(M,S)]=\E_{S\sim\pi}[\log\kappa(M,S)]$
and the analogous equality for $t_0$. Subtracting the baseline expression and using
$H(\pi)-\E_ZH(\pi_Z)=\I_\pi(S:Z)$ yields the claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Residual guarantee under approximate packedness}
\label{app:proof:prop:approximation-guarantee}

\begin{proposition}[Residual guarantee under approximate packedness]
\label{prop:approximation-guarantee}
Suppose the packed log-time law is only approximate, and represent the fixed baseline by a constant signal value $z_0$:
\[
\log \mathsf{T}_{q}^{(M)}(s,z)
=\log\kappa(M)+\log c_{\mathrm{sim}}+\log t_0(M,s)-\log q_z(s)
+r_M(s,z,q),
\]
with $|r_M(s,z,q)|\le\eta$ uniformly over the evaluated systems, modes, signals, and allocations.
For a fixed baseline allocation $q_0$ and a routed allocation $q_Z$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\E\!\left[\log \mathsf{T}_{q_0}^{(M_0)}(S)-\log \mathsf{T}_{q_Z}^{(M)}(S,Z)\right]\\
&\quad=
\log\frac{\kappa(M_0)}{\kappa(M)}
+\varphi(M_0,M)+\I_\pi(S:Z)
+\KL(\pi\|q_0)-\E_Z\KL(\pi_Z^M\|q_Z^M)+R,
\end{aligned}
\]
where
\[
R:=\E[r_{M_0}(S,z_0,q_0)-r_M(S,Z,q_Z)],
\qquad |R|\le 2\eta.
\]
For $q_0=\pi$, this is Theorem~\ref{thm:four-term} plus a residual bounded by $2\eta$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Substitute the approximate log-time law into the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:mode-dependent-cost}, specialized to mode-independent $\kappa(M)$. The ideal terms decompose exactly as before. The remaining difference is
\[
R=\E[r_{M_0}(S,z_0,q_0)-r_M(S,Z,q_Z)].
\]
Therefore
\[
|R|\le \E|r_{M_0}(S,z_0,q_0)|+\E|r_M(S,Z,q_Z)|\le 2\eta.
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{Cost of a Borrowed Allocation}

\begin{proposition}[Cost of a borrowed allocation]
\label{prop:borrowed-allocation}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:model-packed-family}, fix a model $M_B$ and let
$q_Z^{*,B}=\pi_Z^B$.  For any allocation policy $q_Z$ satisfying the standing
support condition,
\[
\E\!\left[
\log\mathsf T_{q_Z}^{(M_B)}(S)
-\log\mathsf T_{q_Z^{*,B}}^{(M_B)}(S)
\right]
=\E_Z\!\left[\KL(\pi_Z^B\|q_Z)\right].
\]
In particular, if $q_Z=q_Z^{*,A}$ and
$q_Z^{*,A}\ne q_Z^{*,B}$ on a set of positive probability, then the excess is
strictly positive whenever the displayed expectation is finite.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Condition on $Z=z$.  The model-dependent cost and competence terms are fixed,
so Lemma~\ref{lem:conditional} gives an excess conditional packed log-time of
$\KL(\pi_z^B\|q_z)$.  Taking expectation over~$Z$ proves the identity.
Nonnegativity of KL divergence, with equality only when its arguments agree,
gives the final claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:model-selection}: Model-selection crossover}
\label{app:proof:cor:model-selection}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:four-term} to both models with the same baseline; subtract.
The baseline terms cancel.
\end{proof}

\section{Retry and Paired-Validation Results}
\label{app:retry-and-validation}

\begin{proposition}[Geometric-retry certified scale]
\label{prop:geometric-certified-scale}
Suppose independent attempts of system $M$ on mode $s$ succeed with probability $p_M(s)\in(0,1)$ and each complete attempt has duration $u_M(s)>0$.
The minimum number of attempts needed to achieve failure probability at most $\delta\in(0,1)$ is
\[
N_M(s,\delta)
=
\left\lceil\frac{\log\delta}{\log(1-p_M(s))}\right\rceil,
\]
and the corresponding focused certified scale is $t_0(M,s,\delta)=u_M(s)N_M(s,\delta)$.
Equivalently, the exact success hazard is $h_M(s):=-\log(1-p_M(s))$; replacing it by $p_M(s)$ is only a small-$p_M(s)$ approximation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
After $N$ independent attempts, failure has probability $(1-p_M(s))^N$.
Thus $(1-p_M(s))^N\le\delta$ if and only if
$N\ge\log\delta/\log(1-p_M(s))$; taking the least integer proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:retry-crossover}: Single-mode retry crossover}
\label{app:proof:retry-crossover}

\begin{proof}
The cheaper system succeeds at least once in $D$ attempts with probability
$1-(1-p_\ell)^D$.
Requiring this quantity to be at least $p_h$ and dividing by the negative number
$\log(1-p_\ell)$ gives
$D\ge\log(1-p_h)/\log(1-p_\ell)$.
The ceiling is therefore the least feasible integer depth.
The fixed-depth cost statement is immediate.
For early stopping, attempt $d$ is reached with probability $(1-p_\ell)^{d-1}$; summing its expected cost gives the finite geometric series.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Finite-horizon first-hit diagnostics]
\label{def:first-hit-diagnostics}
Fix a horizon $H$, action $a$, mode $s$, and censoring floor $p_{\min}\in(0,1)$.
Let $\tau_a$ be first verified success and $C_t(a)$ accumulated deployment cost through time~$t$.
Define
\[
F_a(s,H):=\Prb(\tau_a\le H\mid S=s),
\qquad
C_a(s,H):=\E[C_{\tau_a\wedge H}(a)\mid S=s],
\]
Assume $C_a(s,H)>0$ and define the finite-horizon log-effort diagnostic
\[
\ell_{\mathrm{hit}}(a,s;H)
:=
\log C_a(s,H)-\log(F_a(s,H)\vee p_{\min}).
\]
This empirical surrogate is neither the certified-time quantile $\overline T_A(\delta)$ nor the exact packed time $\mathsf T_q$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:paired-unbiased}: Paired allocation-gain estimator}
\label{app:proof:paired-estimator}

\begin{proof}
Condition on each sampled instance and its two selected actions.
Each repeated-run average has the corresponding conditional expected deployment loss, while $\widehat m_j$ has conditional expectation $m_j$.
The tower property and linearity of expectation then give the stated equality.
\end{proof}
