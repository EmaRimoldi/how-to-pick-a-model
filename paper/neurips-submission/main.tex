\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Which model should be deployed on a new task whose solution can be checked?
Current practice often answers this question indirectly: evaluate candidate
systems end to end on a benchmark and deploy the one with the best aggregate
score.  We call this practice \emph{benchmark racing}.  It has produced
increasingly realistic evaluations, from
repository repair against executable tests to machine-learning engineering
against held-out validation criteria~\citep{jimenez2024swebench,chan2024mlebench}.
Yet an aggregate score is not sufficient for deployment when models
differ in serving cost, can be retried, or exhibit complementary strengths
across instances.  A system that is more accurate per attempt need not be the
one that reaches the first verified solution with the least wall-clock time,
computation, energy, tokens, or money.  Moreover, the same aggregate advantage
can arise from cheaper attempts, greater competence on the relevant kind of
instance, more informative pre-deployment evidence, or better allocation of
effort.  These mechanisms imply different engineering decisions, but an
end-to-end race reports only their net result.

The problem has important antecedents.  Classical algorithm selection maps
instance features to a solver from a portfolio; empirical performance models
predict runtimes on unseen instances; and systems such as SATzilla demonstrate
that the best solver can be instance dependent rather than
global~\citep{rice1976algorithm,hutter2014runtime,xu2012satzilla,kotthoff2016algorithm}.
Statistical racing and best-arm methods reduce the cost of identifying a winner
from repeated evaluations~\citep{maron1993hoeffding,maron1997racing,kaufmann2016bestarm}.
More recently, LLM cascades and routers have learned cost--quality policies over
heterogeneous model pools~\citep{chen2023frugalgpt,ong2024routellm,hu2024routerbench},
while test-time scaling and sample-allocation studies show that model size,
sampling policy, and inference effort interact in task- and effort-dependent
ways~\citep{brown2024monkeys,snell2025testtime,wu2025inference,osca2024}.
These lines of work establish the relevance of per-instance choice and
resource-aware inference.  Their primary output is a runtime predictor,
selector, or allocation policy under a declared performance objective.  Our
focus is complementary: an accounting law for the verified first-passage
resource of a stochastic solver that separates the price of one attempt, its
mode-conditional probability of success, the information available before
deployment, and the extent to which the deployed allocation rule uses that
information.

Our conceptual foundation is the proper-time framework of Achille and
Soatto~\citep{achille2025agents}.  They model an agent as a stochastic dynamical
system acting on verifiable transductive tasks and argue that, because a
universal search procedure may eventually find a valid witness, accuracy alone
does not characterize useful intelligence.  Verification supplies an
observable completion event, so the relevant question is how much resource is
needed to reach it.  Without a finite resource penalty, exhaustive search can
enumerate candidates until one is accepted.  Under the elementary IID retry
idealization with per-attempt success probability \(p>0\), for example,
\[
  \Prob(\text{success by attempt }n)=1-(1-p)^n\longrightarrow1.
\]
Achille and Soatto call this unbounded-compute failure mode \emph{savantry}:
eventual correctness can be obtained by brute force without an efficient,
transferable solution strategy.  We instead require a verified solution within
a predeclared budget and reliability target.  The relevant benefit of learning
is therefore to reduce the resource needed to find a solution.  Their proper time
for reaching a target state is a path quantity,
\(
  \tau_\nu(u\!\to\!v)=\min_{h:u\to v} T(h)/\nu(h\mid u)
\),
which divides trajectory length by the probability of realizing that path.  We
retain two principles from this theory: solutions must be
defined by an external verifier, and information is valuable insofar as it buys
speed.  We do not identify their path proper time with our deployment metric.
Their framework supplies a computational foundation rather than a finite-sample
rule for selecting from a deployed model menu.  For a design~\(d\) and instance
\(x\sim\mu\), we define certified proper time
\(T_d(\delta)\) as the \((1-\delta)\)-quantile of the first-passage proper time
to a verified artifact.  The proper-time measure may be expressed in seconds,
computation, energy, tokens, or money, but it is fixed before comparison and
supplies the unit in which \(T_d(\delta)\) is reported.

For a tractable accounting, we introduce a solver-relevant mode \(S\),
admissible evidence \(Z\), and a physically implemented effort share \(q_Z(S)\).
The PACT hypothesis makes mode-conditioned proper time proportional to unit
attempt cost \(\kappa(d,S)\), focused attempts-to-solution scale
\(t_0(d,S)\), and \(1/q_Z(S)\).  The cost \(\kappa\) inherits the declared unit:
seconds, tokens, energy, or price.
Under a prior-matched baseline, the resulting expected log-proper-time gain is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\mathrm{pack}}
={}&
\underbrace{\mathbb E_S\!\left[\log
  \frac{\kappa(d_0,S)}{\kappa(d,S)}\right]}_{\text{relative unit cost}}
+
\underbrace{\mathbb E_S\!\left[\log
  \frac{t_0(d_0,S)}{t_0(d,S)}\right]}_{\text{focused competence}}
\\[-0.15em]
&+
\underbrace{I(S;Z)}_{\text{mode information}}
-
\underbrace{\mathbb E_Z\!\left[
  \mathrm{KL}(\pi_Z\Vert q_Z)\right]}_{\text{allocation mismatch}}.
\label{eq:intro-four-term}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The identity is exact inside PACT; it is not a general
decomposition of \(\log T_d(\delta)\).  We therefore derive the corresponding
residual form when inverse-share log-linearity is approximate and an exact
geometric-retry bridge showing that, under IID mode-directed attempts, \(1/q\)
governs mean first-passage attempts, whereas certified quantiles require an
explicit correction.  This distinction makes the assumption testable rather
than definitional.

Equation~\eqref{eq:intro-four-term} turns model selection into a diagnostic and
counterfactual calculation: it separates cheaper attempts, model competence,
information acquisition, and allocation quality.  A structured pilot can then
predict held-out expected log first-passage resource, while full deployment
trials remain the confirmation standard.

We make three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0.2em,topsep=0.35em]
  \item Starting from Achille and Soatto's time-centric principle, we formulate
  finite, verifiable model selection through first-passage resource and its
  certified population quantile, while keeping this deployment object distinct
  from both
  Achille--Soatto path proper time and the theorem-facing expected-log
  functional.
  \item We prove the four-term cost--competence--information--mismatch identity,
  its approximate-PACT residual guarantee, and model-selection and retry
  consequences.  Together these results specify which quantities must be
  estimated and the assumptions under which their sum predicts a packed
  expected-log deployment comparison.
  \item We test those assumptions with real language models on a controlled,
  execution-verified coding benchmark.  In a held-out study of 175,104 physical
  trajectories over 768 unseen tasks using Qwen2.5-Coder 7B and 14B, the
  inverse-share exponent is \(0.987\), the four-term prediction has residual RMS
  \(0.015\) nat, and its model choice agrees with the held-out resource oracle in
  all six conditions.  Because a strict residual omnibus rejects exact closure,
  we interpret the decomposition as a falsifiable decision approximation, not
  a universal equality.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Verifier-Backed Model Selection}
\label{sec:problem}

We first separate the deployment quantity that the builder ultimately cares
about from the expected-log functional that admits the decomposition.  This
distinction fixes the task, the proper-time unit, and the statistical estimand
before introducing latent modes.

\begin{definition}[Verifiable task family and proper-time measure]
\label{def:task-family}
A \emph{verifiable task family} is a tuple
\(
  \mathrm{T}=(\mathrm{X},\mathrm{Y},V,\mu)
\).  Each \(x\in\mathrm{X}\) is a concrete problem instance: the
observed input and context for which one artifact \(y\in\mathrm{Y}\) must be
constructed.  The distribution \(\mu\) describes future instances, while the
predeclared external verifier
\(V:\mathrm{X}\times\mathrm{Y}\to\{0,1\}\) supplies the common success
criterion: \(V(x,y)=1\) is the observable event that instance \(x\) has been
completed.  A \emph{proper-time measure} assigns every partial execution a
nonnegative, additive budget coordinate \(B\).  It may be measured in isolated
GPU-seconds, joules, tokens, dollars, or a predeclared scalarization of several
resources. 
\end{definition}

A \emph{deployment design} \(d\) fixes the complete solution procedure: backbone model
and implementation, prompting and decoding, retry or allocation, and verifier invocation.
Given instance \(x\), let \(y(B;x)\) be the generally random artifact available
after cumulative budget \(B\).  Its law is induced by \(d\); throughout, the
dependence of \(y\) on the design and its internal randomness is implicit.  All
probabilities below are taken jointly over \(x\sim\mu\) and this randomness.

\begin{definition}[Verified first passage]
\label{def:first-passage}
For a design \(d\) and instance \(x\), define the verified first-passage budget
\begin{equation}
\tau_d(x)
:=
\begin{cases}
\inf\{B\ge0:V(x,y(B;x))=1\},
  & \text{if a verified artifact is reached},\\
\infty, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:first-passage}
\end{equation}
Thus \(\tau_d(x)\) is the realized budget consumed before the first
verified artifact.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Certified proper time]
\label{def:certified-time}
For failure tolerance \(\delta\in(0,1)\), define
\begin{equation}
T_d(\delta)
:=\inf\{B\ge0:
\Prob[\tau_d(x)\le B]\ge1-\delta\}.
\label{eq:proper-time-system}
\end{equation}
The certified proper time \(T_d(\delta)\) is the population
\((1-\delta)\)-quantile of the verified first passage: the smallest proper
budget by which a verified artifact is produced with probability at least
\(1-\delta\) over deployment instances and repeated runs.  Its unit is
inherited from the chosen proper-time measure.
For a baseline \(d_0\), the certified log-speedup is
\(\Delta_{\mathrm{cert}}(d_0,d;\delta)
=\log T_{d_0}(\delta)-\log T_d(\delta)\), whenever both quantiles are finite
and positive.
\end{definition}

% \(\tau_i:=\tau_d(x_i)\), with unsuccessful runs recorded as
% \(\tau_i=\infty\).  The empirical first-passage curve and proper-time estimator
% are
% \begin{equation}
%   \widehat F_d(B)
%   :=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1\{\tau_i\le B\},
%   \qquad
%   \widehat T_d(\delta)

%   \label{eq:proper-time-estimator}

\begin{problem}[Model selection]
\label{prob:model-selection}
Given a finite, predeclared design menu \(\mathcal D\), a task family, a common
proper-time measure, failure tolerance \(\delta\), and budget \(B\), first form
the admissible set
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal D_{\mathrm{adm}}(\delta,B)
  :=
  \left\{
    d\in\mathcal D:
    \Prob[\tau_d(x)\le B]\ge1-\delta
  \right\}.
  \label{eq:admissible-designs}
\end{equation}
Then select
\begin{equation}
  d^\star
  \in
  \argmin_{d\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{adm}}(\delta,B)}
  T_d(\delta).
  \label{eq:deployment-selection}
\end{equation}
Thus reliability is declared and tested before proper time ranks the
admissible candidates.
Candidates must share the proper-time measure and verifier.  Repeating the
analysis in another common unit gives another proper-time comparison;
simultaneous resources require a scalarization or Pareto frontier.
\end{problem}

\subsection{Task modes, actions, and PACT allocation}
\label{sec:packed-setup}

\begin{definition}[Task modes and task information]
\label{def:packed-objects}
Fix the verifiable task family
\(\mathrm{T}\) from
Definition~\ref{def:task-family}.  The finite set
\(\mathcal S=\{1,\ldots,m\}\) indexes predeclared, solver-relevant subfamilies
of the instance space \(\mathrm{X}\).  For a task instance
\(x\sim\mu\), the random variable \(S\in\mathcal S\) records which subfamily
generated \(x\). Formally,
\[
  x\mid S=s\sim\mu_s,
  \qquad
  \pi(s):=\Prob[S=s],
  \qquad
  \mu=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\pi(s)\mu_s.
\]
Here \(\pi\) is the prior frequency of the subfamilies in deployment and
\(\mu_s\) is the distribution of task instances within subfamily \(s\).  A
mode therefore means shared structure among instances of the same task family,
such as a dependency pattern, proof schema, edit locality, or algorithmic
invariant.  The subfamilies are fixed. 

For any design \(d\), the mode-conditioned proper time is
\begin{equation}
  T_d(\delta\mid s)
  :=
  \inf\left\{
    B\ge0:
    \Prob[\tau_d(x)\le B\mid S=s]\ge1-\delta
  \right\}.
  \label{eq:mode-proper-time}
\end{equation}
% The overall \(T_d(\delta)\) is the quantile of the mixture over modes.

A \emph{task-information signal} is a random variable
\(Z\in\mathcal Z\), where \(\mathcal Z\) is the space of possible signal
values. When the design observes the realized signal
value \(z\), its uncertainty about the instance's latent mode changes from the
prior \(\pi\) to the posterior
\[
  \pi_z(s):=\Prob[S=s\mid Z=z].
\]
The signal may be informative, \(I(S;Z)>0\), or uninformative,
\(Z\perp S\).  Its intended role is to guide the compute allocation toward the
actions appropriate for the current mode. 
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Fixed-backbone actions and PACT allocator]
\label{def:packed-allocator}
Fix a design \(d\) with backbone model \(M_d\).  Its predeclared action bank is
\[
  \mathcal A_d
  :=\{a_{d,s}:s\in\mathcal S\}
\] 
All actions within \(d\) share the
same backbone. Each action is intended to be seen as a specific model configureation / harness / .. .che sia specifica per risolvere in task mode preciso. \(s\).
Let \(\Delta(\mathcal S):=\{q\in[0,1]^m:\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}q(s)=1\}\)
denote the probability simplex over modes. A \emph{PACT allocator} is a map
\(q_d:\mathcal Z\longrightarrow\Delta(\mathcal S)\), written
\(z\longmapsto q_z\).

A \emph{focused mode-\(s\) attempt} is one execution of \(a_{d,s}\).  Let
\(t_0(d,s)>0\) be the expected number of such focused attempts to the first
verified artifact, and let \(\kappa(d,s)>0\) be the expected cost of one global
attempt on a mode-\(s\) instance in the declared proper-time unit. Exact packedness requires
this attempt-cost scale to be invariant to the allocation; departures caused by
action-dependent lengths or overhead are covered by
Proposition~\ref{prop:approx-packed}.
\end{definition}

For two mode distributions \(a,b\in\Delta(\mathcal S)\), write
\[
  H(a):=-\sum_s a(s)\log a(s),
  \qquad
  \KL(a\Vert b):=\sum_s a(s)\log\frac{a(s)}{b(s)},
\]
with \(0\log0:=0\).  Here \(H(a)\) is the Shannon entropy, measuring uncertainty
about the mode in nats, and \(\KL(a\Vert b)\) is the nonnegative mismatch from
using mode distribution \(b\) in place of \(a\).  The mutual information
\[
  I(S;Z)
  :=H(\pi)-\E_ZH(\pi_Z)
  =\E_Z\!\left[\KL(\pi_Z\Vert\pi)\right]
\]
is the expected reduction in mode uncertainty after the design observes the
task information, where \(\pi_Z\) is the posterior evaluated at the random
observation \(Z\).  
% It is zero when \(Z\) and \(S\) are independent and reaches
% \(H(\pi)\) when \(Z\) identifies the latent mode almost surely.  In this sense,
% \(Z\) is the finite-mode analogue of the data or prompt that conditions the
% search distribution in Achille and Soatto's ``information is speed''
% result~\citep{achille2025agents}.  Their quantity is algorithmic mutual
% information between data and a successful trajectory; ours is the Shannon
% mutual information between task information and a predeclared finite mode, a
% finite and experimentally estimable counterpart rather than the same object.

\begin{assumption}[PACT inverse-share law]
\label{ass:packed}
Fix a task mode \(s\) and an observed signal value \(z\).  Every action attempts
to produce a verified artifact, but \(a_{d,s}\) is specialized for mode \(s\)
and is expected to reach verification with less proper budget than the other
actions.  The exact PACT law models the regime in which this specialized action
dominates mode-\(s\) first passage.  The allocator gives \(a_{d,s}\) the
executed share \(q_z(s)\), while the cost scale of a global attempt remains
\(\kappa(d,s)\).  The resulting mode-conditioned packed proper time is
\begin{equation}
  T_{d,q}^{\mathrm{pack}}(s,z)
  =\kappa(d,s)\frac{t_0(d,s)}{q_z(s)}.
  \label{eq:packed-law}
\end{equation}
The factor \(1/q_z(s)\) therefore states that assigning only a fraction
\(q_z(s)\) of attempts to \(a_{d,s}\) dilates the specialized
attempts-to-solution by the inverse share.  Other actions may still reach a
verified solution on mode-\(s\) instances, but theoretically require more
proper budget.  Non-negligible cross-mode success, interference, or changes in
the slot-cost scale make the exact law depart from observed proper time and
must enter the residual model below.
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[PACT expected log-proper-time]
\label{def:packed-risk}
For the joint law of \((S,Z)\), define
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L(d,q):=\E[\log T_{d,q}^{\mathrm{pack}}(S,Z)].
  \label{eq:packed-risk}
\end{equation}
For a baseline design \(d_0\) with the baseline allocation \(q_0\), define
\(
  \Delta_{\mathrm{pack}}
  :=\mathcal L(d_0,q_0)-\mathcal L(d,q)
\).
We require \(q_0(s)>0\) whenever \(\pi(s)>0\).
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Metric and identifiability guardrails]
\label{rem:guardrails}
In general,
\(
  \Delta_{\mathrm{pack}}
  \ne\Delta_{\mathrm{cert}}(d_0,d;\delta)
\), and \(\E[\log T]\ne\log\E[T]\).  The theorem concerns packed expected
log-proper-time; \(T_d(\delta)\) is a population quantile.  The IID geometric
bridge in Section~\ref{sec:quantile-bridge} connects them, but otherwise they
must be reported separately.  Total proper time identifies only
\(\kappa(d,s)t_0(d,s)\); separating relative unit cost from competence requires
attempt-level measurement of \(\kappa\) in the declared unit.
\end{remark}

\section{Four-Term PACT Proper-Time Accounting}
\label{sec:accounting}

The decomposition follows from the conditional cross-entropy incurred by the
physical allocation.  Complete proofs of every result in this section and
Section~\ref{sec:quantile-bridge} appear in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\begin{lemma}[Conditional log-proper-time and posterior matching]
\label{lem:conditional}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:packed}, for any signal value \(z\), write
\(\E_z[\cdot]:=\E[\cdot\mid Z=z]\).  Then
\begin{equation}
\E_z[\log T_{d,q}^{\mathrm{pack}}(S,z)]
=\E_z[\log(\kappa(d,S)t_0(d,S))]+H(\pi_z)+\KL(\pi_z\Vert q_z).
\label{eq:conditional-decomposition}
\end{equation}
Consequently, \(q_z=\pi_z\) uniquely minimizes conditional expected
log-proper-time, and its excess over the minimum is exactly
\(\KL(\pi_z\Vert q_z)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{corollary}[Uninformative signal]
\label{cor:uninformative-signal}
If \(Z\perp S\), then \(\pi_z=\pi\) almost surely and \(I(S;Z)=0\).
Consequently, the PACT-optimal allocation is the fixed prior allocation
\(q_z=\pi\).  Any other supported allocation incurs the nonnegative penalty
\[
  \E_Z\KL(\pi\Vert q_Z)
\]
without an information gain.  Thus an uninformative signal cannot improve
expected log proper time through the information channel.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[PACT four-term expected-log proper-time decomposition]
\label{thm:four-term}
Let \((d_0,q_0)\) use the baseline allocation \(q_0\) and let \((d,q)\) use signal
\(Z\).  Under Assumption~\ref{ass:packed}, define
\begin{align*}
  C(d_0,d)&:=\E_S\!\left[\log\frac{\kappa(d_0,S)}{\kappa(d,S)}\right],
  &
  \Phi(d_0,d)&:=\E_S\!\left[\log\frac{t_0(d_0,S)}{t_0(d,S)}\right],\\
  G(d)&:=I(S;Z),
  &
  \varepsilon(d,q)&:=\E_Z[\KL(\pi_Z\Vert q_Z)].
\end{align*}
Then
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
  \Delta_{\mathrm{pack}}
  =C(d_0,d)+\Phi(d_0,d)+G(d)
   +\KL(\pi\Vert q_0)-\varepsilon(d,q).}
  \label{eq:four-term-general}
\end{equation}
For the prior-matched baseline \(q_0=\pi\), the baseline mismatch vanishes,
leaving the four terms \emph{unit cost}, \emph{focused competence},
\emph{mode information}, and \emph{allocation mismatch}.
\end{theorem}

The identity is exact because Assumption~\ref{ass:packed} makes log-proper-time
additive in these quantities.  Its value is diagnostic: it turns an aggregate
gain into separately measurable mechanisms and exposes the assumption that
must survive held-out residual tests.

\begin{corollary}[Design-selection score]
\label{cor:model-score}
For candidate designs sharing \((\pi,\mathcal S)\), possibly with
candidate-specific signals \(Z_d\), posteriors
\(\pi_z^d(s):=\Prob[S=s\mid Z_d=z]\), and allocations \(q^d\), define
\begin{equation}
\mathcal J(d,q^d):=\E_S[\log(\kappa(d,S)t_0(d,S))]
-I(S;Z_d)+\E_{Z_d}[\KL(\pi^{d}_{Z_d}\Vert q^d_{Z_d})].
\label{eq:model-score}
\end{equation}
Then \(\mathcal L(d,q^d)=H(\pi)+\mathcal J(d,q^d)\).
Thus the packed-optimal candidate is any minimizer of \(\mathcal J\).
\end{corollary}

Exact packedness is not needed for a useful decision, but its failure must be
quantified rather than hidden.

\begin{proposition}[Approximate packedness]
\label{prop:approx-packed}
Let \(r_d(s,z,q)\in\mathbb R\) be the scalar cellwise log-residual, and let
\(\eta\ge0\) be a uniform upper bound on its magnitude, measured in nats.
Suppose the actual mode-conditioned scale satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \log\widetilde T_{d,q}(s,z)
  =\log T_{d,q}^{\mathrm{pack}}(s,z)+r_d(s,z,q),
  \qquad |r_d(s,z,q)|\le\eta
  \label{eq:approx-packed}
\end{equation}
uniformly for both compared designs.  Define
\(
\widetilde{\mathcal L}(d,q):=\E[\log\widetilde T_{d,q}(S,Z)]
\)
and
\(
\widetilde\Delta:=
\widetilde{\mathcal L}(d_0,q_0)-\widetilde{\mathcal L}(d,q)
\).  For a prior-matched baseline,
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde\Delta
  =\underbrace{C+\Phi+G-\varepsilon}_{\Delta_4}+R,
  \qquad
  R=\E[r_{d_0}(S,q_0)-r_d(S,Z,q)],
  \qquad |R|\le2\eta.
  \label{eq:residual-decomposition}
\end{equation}
Here \(R\in\mathbb R\) is the scalar difference between the expected baseline
and candidate residuals, so \(|R|\) is the ordinary absolute value of a scalar,
not a vector norm.  The bound \(2\eta\) follows because each of the two
cellwise residuals has magnitude at most \(\eta\).
The term \(r_{d_0}(s,q_0)\) has no signal argument because \(q_0\) is fixed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[Residual-aware decision margin]
\label{cor:robust-margin}
Let \(\widehat\Delta_4\) estimate the four-term prediction with
\(|\widehat\Delta_4-\Delta_4|\le e\), and suppose \(|R|\le\rho\).
Then \(\widetilde\Delta>0\) whenever
\(\widehat\Delta_4>e+\rho\), and \(\widetilde\Delta<0\) whenever
\(\widehat\Delta_4<-e-\rho\).  Inside the band
\(|\widehat\Delta_4|\le e+\rho\), the accounting does not certify a winner.
\end{corollary}

% and structural residual are both charged before changing the deployed model.

\section{From First-Passage Means to Certified Quantiles}
\label{sec:quantile-bridge}

The inverse-share factor \(1/q\) has an exact first-passage interpretation, but
the interpretation depends on whether the target is a mean or a quantile.  The
bridge below isolates the limiting case in which off-mode success is
negligible.  If an off-mode attempt instead succeeds with probability \(r\),
the per-slot success probability becomes \(qp+(1-q)r\), attenuating the
inverse-share effect.

\begin{theorem}[Geometric allocation bridge]
\label{thm:geometric-bridge}
Fix one mode.  In each global slot, independently allocate a mode-directed attempt
to the true mode with probability \(q\in(0,1]\).  A true-mode attempt succeeds
independently with probability \(p\in(0,1)\), and off-mode attempts cannot
succeed.  If \(N_{p,q}\) is the first successful global slot, then
\begin{equation}
  N_{p,q}\sim\operatorname{Geom}(pq),
  \qquad
  \E[N_{p,q}]=\frac{1}{pq}.
  \label{eq:geometric-mean}
\end{equation}
Its certified slot quantile is
\begin{equation}
  Q_{p,q}(\delta)
  =\left\lceil\frac{\log\delta}{\log(1-pq)}\right\rceil.
  \label{eq:geometric-quantile}
\end{equation}
Let \(h(x):=-\log(1-x)\) and remove the integer ceiling, so
\(\widetilde Q_{p,q}(\delta)=-\log\delta/h(pq)\).  Comparing this quantity with
the inverse-share extrapolation of its focused value,
\(\widetilde Q_{p,1}(\delta)/q\), gives the log residual
\begin{equation}
  r_{\mathrm{inv}}(p,q)
  :=\log\frac{\widetilde Q_{p,q}(\delta)}
                    {\widetilde Q_{p,1}(\delta)/q}
  =\log\frac{q\,h(p)}{h(pq)},
  \qquad
  0\le r_{\mathrm{inv}}(p,q)\le-\log(1-p).
  \label{eq:quantile-residual}
\end{equation}
For the unrounded quantile \(\widetilde Q_{p,q}\), ceiling contributes at most
\begin{equation}
  0\le\log Q_{p,q}(\delta)-\log\widetilde Q_{p,q}(\delta)
  <\log\!\left(1+\frac{1}{\widetilde Q_{p,q}(\delta)}\right).
  \label{eq:ceiling-residual}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Thus \(1/q\) is exact for mean first passage and a controlled approximation for
a cellwise quantile whose log residual vanishes as \(p\to0\).  For heterogeneous
modes or success
probabilities, the population quantity in Definition~\ref{def:certified-time}
is a mixture quantile; it cannot in general be obtained by averaging cellwise
quantiles.  Accordingly, \(t_0^{\mathrm{mean}}=1/p\) and the focused quantile
in~\eqref{eq:geometric-quantile} are different estimands and must not be
interchanged.

\begin{corollary}[Single-mode retry crossover]
\label{cor:retry-crossover}
Let a high-cost system have one-attempt success \(p_h\) and cost \(\kappa_h\),
and a lower-cost system have \(0<p_\ell<p_h<1\) and per-attempt cost
\(\kappa_\ell\).  The minimum retry depth whose fixed block matches or exceeds
the high-cost system's reliability is
\begin{equation}
  D_\times
  =\left\lceil\frac{\log(1-p_h)}{\log(1-p_\ell)}\right\rceil.
  \label{eq:retry-crossover}
\end{equation}
The lower-cost block is cheaper when \(D_\times\kappa_\ell<\kappa_h\).  If it
stops at first success, its expected cost through depth \(D\) is instead
\begin{equation}
  \kappa_\ell\frac{1-(1-p_\ell)^D}{p_\ell}.
  \label{eq:stopped-retry-cost}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We ask four questions.  First, does reliability-first ranking differ from a
single-attempt or model-size ranking?  Second, is the inverse-share response
close enough to \(1/q\) for the PACT accounting to be decision-useful?  Third,
do the independently estimated four terms predict held-out expected log
resource?  Fourth, when the accounting fails, does its residual identify a
concrete deployment failure such as cross-mode success or allocation-dependent
cost?  The unit of analysis is a task instance, not an individual retry.

controlled SAI-3 experiment is the identification study: its three modes are
mutually incompatible hidden API contracts, and its three mode-directed
actions expose only the corresponding evidence shard.  HumanEval+ and MBPP+

\subsection{Estimands and statistical reporting}

Every run stops at its first verified artifact or at the predeclared horizon
\(B_{\max}\).  We record the event indicator, attempts, generated tokens,
generation and verification time, and cumulative resource.  For common
administrative censoring, the empirical success curve is
\(
\widehat F_d(B)=N_x^{-1}\sum_i
\mathbf 1\{\tau_{d,i}\le B\}
\); with unequal horizons we use the Kaplan--Meier product-limit estimator
instead~\citep{kaplan1958nonparametric}.  We report
\(\widehat T_d(\delta)=\inf\{B:\widehat F_d(B)\ge1-\delta\}\) only when the
target is reached.  Otherwise it is right-censored, not extrapolated.

Our primary reliability target is \(1-\delta=0.90\).  Following standard
empirical reporting practice, tables give the point estimate, its standard
error in parentheses, the number of independent instances, and the exact
resampling level.  Confidence intervals for proper-time quantiles invert the
bootstrap success curve, in the spirit of survival-quantile inversion
methods~\citep{brookmeyer1982confidence}; other nonlinear statistics use a
nonparametric instance-block bootstrap~\citep{efron1993bootstrap}.  All retries,
models, and allocation conditions for a sampled instance move together in one
bootstrap block.  SAI-3 uses 5,000 bootstrap replications; public frontiers use
1,000; synthetic proper-time quantiles use 2,000 paired instance-bootstrap
replications.  Simulation standard errors are the paired trial standard
deviation divided by \(\sqrt{10{,}000}\).  We report effect sizes and intervals
rather than using significance stars as a substitute for engineering relevance.

The SAI-3 baseline is Qwen2.5-Coder-14B with prior-matched allocation; the
deployed candidate is Qwen2.5-Coder-7B under six frozen signal--allocation

channel, and \(q_z\); confirmation opens 768 fresh tasks only after these
objects and the analysis are frozen.  The public frontier executes 1.5B, 7B,
and 32B Qwen2.5-Coder on HumanEval+ and MBPP+ and 1.5B and 7B Qwen2.5 on BBH,

calibrated simulation compares the pairs shown in

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Controlled confirmation: accurate decisions, rejected exactness}

The SAI-3 confirmation contains 175,104 physical trajectories and no censoring
or wrong-shard success.  Regressing held-out mean log first-passage resource on
\(-\log q\) gives an inverse-share exponent of \(0.9868\), with 90\% bootstrap
interval \([0.9784,0.9952]\).  Across the six primary contrasts, the mean
four-term residual is \(0.0100\) nat, its RMS is \(0.0147\) nat, and the 95\%
bootstrap upper bound on RMS is \(0.0350\) nat.  The predicted design agrees
with the held-out resource oracle in all six conditions; the minimum bootstrap
selection probability is \(1.000\), and estimated oracle regret is zero.

Exact closure nevertheless fails its frozen omnibus criterion.  Residuals
increase with allocation mismatch with slope \(0.0377\)
(95\% interval \([0.0108,0.0647]\), Holm-adjusted \(p=0.0224\)).  This is about
\(0.0299\) nat, or 3.0\% in multiplicative resource, at the largest tested
mismatch.  A fresh 122,880-trajectory diagnostic raises repetitions per task
cell from 6 to 32: the mismatch slope falls to \(0.0150\)
(95\% interval \([-0.0098,0.0394]\), adjusted \(p=0.487\)), while the
inverse-share exponent is \(0.9944\) (90\% interval \([0.9865,1.0021]\)).
This attenuation supports finite-repetition bias as a mechanism, but does not
reverse the preregistered rejection.  The defensible result is therefore
\emph{decision-accurate approximate accounting}, not an empirical proof of an
exact universal identity.

\subsection{Observed public frontiers: reliability is often unattained}

The observed ten-retry frontiers provide a necessary negative control.  On
HumanEval+, the best final verified rate is 149/164 for the 7B model,
90.9\% with 95\% Wilson interval \([85.5,94.4]\%\)~\citep{wilson1927probable};
its descriptive empirical
\(T_{90}\) is 736 generated tokens (38.27 seconds), but a finite 95\% interval
is not identified because the lower reliability bound misses the target.  The other HumanEval+
models do not reach 90\%.  The best MBPP+ rate is 323/378 for 32B,
85.4\% \([81.5,88.7]\%\), and the best BBH rate is 951/1,200 for 7B,
79.3\% \([76.9,81.5]\%\).  Their \(T_{90}\) values are therefore censored.
Moreover, even HumanEval+ 7B does not meet the conservative admissibility rule
based on the lower confidence bound.  The practical conclusion is not to
declare another model the winner, but to increase the horizon, reduce the
reliability target prospectively, or report that the candidate menu is
inadmissible.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The results support reliability-first selection, not a universal winner.
Freeze the verifier, resource clock, \(B\), and \(\delta\); admit \(d\) only if
a lower confidence bound on \(\Prob(\tau_d\le B)\) reaches \(1-\delta\); then
minimize held-out \(\widehat T_d(\delta)\).  Replace the incumbent only if the
paired interval or residual-aware margin in
Corollary~\ref{cor:robust-margin} excludes zero.

The terms prescribe interventions: negative cost calls for better serving;
negative competence for another backbone or action; low information for a
better signal; and high mismatch for a better allocator.  A large residual
invalidates the surrogate and returns the decision to direct measurement.
Proper time selects; four-term accounting audits the mechanism.

The BBH reversal shows that average log resource and SLA-like \(T_{90}\) can
disagree.  The objective must be declared before observing results; the full
curve exposes this tradeoff and permits reranking at another reliability level
without rerunning inference.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The four-dataset accounting is calibrated simulation: it validates estimators,
power, and figure design, not executed-router performance.  Its signal, action
overlap, and BBH cells require held-out randomized runs.  Physical SAI-3 uses a
synthetic benchmark, one dense family, fixed-length slots, and nearly zero
off-mode success; external validity is narrow, and exact closure fails even
there.

HumanEval+ and MBPP+ modes are length strata, not learned solver structures;
BBH lacks a 32B run; and cells below 90\% after ten attempts remain censored,
without finite \(T_{90}\).  Modes, actions, and routers must be frozen on
calibration data and evaluated on disjoint instances.

IID retries, stationary hazards, and allocation-invariant costs are
approximations: history, correlation, caching, batching, verifier latency, and
adaptive stopping can change \(t_0\) and \(\kappa\).  Charge signal acquisition
to the same clock.  Verification certifies only the declared criterion, not
usefulness or safety.  If resources cannot be credibly scalarized, report a
Pareto frontier rather than one ranking.

\section{Proofs}

\subsection{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:conditional}}

\begin{proof}
Taking logs in~\eqref{eq:packed-law} and conditioning on \(Z=z\) gives
\begin{align*}
\E[\log T_{d,q}^{\mathrm{pack}}(S,z)\mid Z=z]
={}&\E[\log(\kappa(d,S)t_0(d,S))\mid Z=z]\\
&+\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\pi_z(s)(-\log q_z(s)).
\end{align*}
The final sum is the cross-entropy between \(\pi_z\) and \(q_z\), and
\begin{align*}
\sum_s\pi_z(s)(-\log q_z(s))
&=-\sum_s\pi_z(s)\log\pi_z(s)
  +\sum_s\pi_z(s)\log\frac{\pi_z(s)}{q_z(s)}\\
&=H(\pi_z)+\KL(\pi_z\Vert q_z).
\end{align*}
For completeness, Jensen's inequality for \(-\log\) gives
\[
\KL(\pi_z\Vert q_z)
=\E_{\pi_z}\!\left[-\log\frac{q_z(S)}{\pi_z(S)}\right]
\ge-\log\!\left(\sum_{s:\pi_z(s)>0}q_z(s)\right)\ge0.
\]
Equality requires all mass to lie on the posterior support and
\(q_z(s)/\pi_z(s)\) to be constant there, hence \(q_z=\pi_z\).  The support
condition in Definition~\ref{def:packed-allocator} makes every displayed
quantity finite.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:four-term}}

\begin{proof}
For the deployed design, Lemma~\ref{lem:conditional} and the tower property
give
\begin{align}
\mathcal L(d,q)
={}&\E_S[\log\kappa(d,S)]
 +\E_S[\log t_0(d,S)]\nonumber\\
&+\E_Z[H(\pi_Z)]+\varepsilon(d,q).
\label{eq:proof-deployed-risk}
\end{align}
The baseline allocation has cross-entropy
\begin{equation}
  \E_S[-\log q_0(S)]=H(\pi)+\KL(\pi\Vert q_0),
  \label{eq:proof-baseline-crossentropy}
\end{equation}
and hence
\begin{align}
\mathcal L(d_0,q_0)
={}&\E_S[\log\kappa(d_0,S)]
 +\E_S[\log t_0(d_0,S)]\nonumber\\
&+H(\pi)+\KL(\pi\Vert q_0).
\label{eq:proof-baseline-risk}
\end{align}
Subtracting~\eqref{eq:proof-deployed-risk} from
\eqref{eq:proof-baseline-risk}, and using
\(
I(S;Z)=H(\pi)-\E_ZH(\pi_Z)
\), yields~\eqref{eq:four-term-general}.  If \(q_0=\pi\), then
\(\KL(\pi\Vert q_0)=0\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:model-score}}

\begin{proof}
Apply~\eqref{eq:proof-deployed-risk} to each candidate and substitute
\(
\E_{Z_d}H(\pi^d_{Z_d})=H(\pi)-I(S;Z_d)
\).  The term \(H(\pi)\) is common to every candidate; the remaining terms are
exactly \(\mathcal J(d,q^d)\).  Therefore the
minimizers of \(\mathcal L\) and \(\mathcal J\) coincide.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:approx-packed}}

\begin{proof}
Substitute~\eqref{eq:approx-packed} into the expected log-proper-time difference.
The ideal terms equal \(\Delta_4\) by Theorem~\ref{thm:four-term}; the remaining
terms are
\(
R=\E[r_{d_0}(S,q_0)-r_d(S,Z,q)]
\).  The triangle inequality gives
\[
  |R|
  \le\E|r_{d_0}(S,q_0)|+\E|r_d(S,Z,q)|
  \le2\eta.
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:robust-margin}}

\begin{proof}
Write \(a=\widehat\Delta_4-\Delta_4\).  Then
\(
\widetilde\Delta=\widehat\Delta_4-a+R
\), where \(|a|\le e\) and \(|R|\le\rho\).  Hence
\[
  \widehat\Delta_4-e-\rho
  \le\widetilde\Delta
  \le\widehat\Delta_4+e+\rho.
\]
The two sign claims follow from the corresponding strict endpoint inequality.
If the interval contains zero, these bounds alone cannot determine the sign.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:geometric-bridge}}

\begin{proof}
A global slot succeeds exactly when it is allocated to the true mode and that
mode-directed attempt succeeds.  Independence therefore gives per-slot success
probability \(pq\), so
\[
  \Prob[N_{p,q}=n]=(1-pq)^{n-1}pq,
  \qquad
  \Prob[N_{p,q}>n]=(1-pq)^n.
\]
The survival-sum formula yields
\[
  \E[N_{p,q}]
  =\sum_{n\ge0}\Prob[N_{p,q}>n]
  =\sum_{n\ge0}(1-pq)^n
  =\frac{1}{pq}.
\]
Moreover,
\(
\Prob[N_{p,q}\le n]=1-(1-pq)^n
\).  Requiring this probability to be at least \(1-\delta\) gives
\(
(1-pq)^n\le\delta
\), which is equivalent to
\(
n\ge\log\delta/\log(1-pq)
\); taking the least integer proves~\eqref{eq:geometric-quantile}.

Let \(h(x)=-\log(1-x)\).  Convexity of \(h\), together with \(h(0)=0\), gives
\(h(qp)\le qh(p)\), proving the lower residual bound.  Also,
\[
  h(x)=\int_0^x\frac{dt}{1-t}
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  x\le h(x)\le\frac{x}{1-x}.
\]
Therefore
\(
q h(p)/h(qp)\le q[p/(1-p)]/(pq)=1/(1-p)
\), proving the upper residual bound.  Finally, for every \(u>0\),
\(u\le\lceil u\rceil<u+1\); applying \(\log\) with
\(u=\widetilde Q_{p,q}(\delta)\) proves~\eqref{eq:ceiling-residual}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:retry-crossover}}

\begin{proof}
After \(D\) independent low-cost attempts, failure probability is
\((1-p_\ell)^D\), so reliability is \(1-(1-p_\ell)^D\).  Matching the high-cost
one-attempt reliability requires
\[
  1-(1-p_\ell)^D\ge p_h
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  D\ge\frac{\log(1-p_h)}{\log(1-p_\ell)}.
\]
The least integer is~\eqref{eq:retry-crossover}; multiplying by
\(\kappa_\ell\) gives the fixed-block cost condition.  With stopping, attempt
\(d\) is incurred only if the first \(d-1\) attempts fail, which has probability
\((1-p_\ell)^{d-1}\).  Therefore
\[
  \E[\text{cost through depth }D]
  =\kappa_\ell\sum_{d=1}^{D}(1-p_\ell)^{d-1}
  =\kappa_\ell\frac{1-(1-p_\ell)^D}{p_\ell}.
\]
\end{proof}
