\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We ask four questions: whether direct proper-time measurement rejects
unreliable candidates; whether tool allocation follows inverse-share
scaling; whether independently estimated four-term mechanisms predict held-out
resource; and whether conclusions transport beyond the controlled setting.

\subsection{Benchmarks, models, and evidence classes}

Table~\ref{tab:experimental-design} summarizes four benchmarks.  SAI-3 is a
procedural sharded API-integration benchmark with three hidden, mutually
incompatible Python contracts.  Each action receives the common task and one
contract-specific evidence shard; the verifier combines static-policy checks,
hidden differential tests, metamorphic tests, and a mutation-score gate.  This
construction creates a controlled action-success matrix without revealing the
correct evidence shard.  HumanEval+ and MBPP+ provide execution-verified code
generation tasks~\citep{chen2021humaneval,liu2023evalplus,austin2021mbpp}; their
three reported classes are precomputed tertiles of reference-program length,
not claims of latent semantic structure.  BBH supplies five cognitive families
(algorithmic, arithmetic, language, logical deduction, and spatial--temporal)
\citep{suzgun2023bbh}.  Coding tasks use 1.5B, 7B, and 32B Qwen2.5-Coder
checkpoints where available; BBH uses the corresponding general Qwen2.5 1.5B
and 7B checkpoints because its tasks are not code generation
\citep{hui2024qwen25coder,yang2024qwen25}.

Every run stops at its first verified artifact or at the predeclared horizon
\(B\).  We record the event indicator, attempts, generated tokens, generation
and verification time, and cumulative resource.  We report
\(\widehat T_d(\delta)=\inf\{B:\widehat F_d(B)\ge1-\delta\}\) only when the target is reached. Otherwise it is right-censored, not extrapolated. Our primary reliability target is \(1-\delta=0.90\).  Following standard
empirical reporting practice, tables give the point estimate, its standard
error in parentheses, the number of independent instances, and the exact
resampling level.  All retries,
models, and allocation conditions for a sampled instance move together in one
bootstrap block.  SAI-3 uses 5,000 bootstrap replications; public frontiers use
1,000; synthetic proper-time quantiles use 2,000 paired instance-bootstrap
replications.  Simulation standard errors are the paired trial standard
deviation divided by \(\sqrt{10{,}000}\).  We report effect sizes and intervals
rather than using significance stars as a substitute for engineering relevance.

\subsection{Frozen comparisons}

The SAI-3 baseline is Qwen2.5-Coder-14B with prior-matched allocation; the
deployed candidate is Qwen2.5-Coder-7B under six frozen signal--allocation
conditions.  Its calibration split contains 768 distinct tasks, 256 per correct
action,
disjoint from confirmation.  For each task and model, 64 matched-action attempts
and four attempts per wrong action estimate focused success and off-reference
hazard; attempt ledgers estimate mean retry cost \(\overline K\).  The controlled signal channel and
allocations are protocol constants rather than learned quantities.  A separate
practical-router diagnostic fits multinomial logistic regression on stack-trace
tokens and package metadata and estimates its posterior by a five-fold
out-of-fold confusion matrix.  Confirmation opens 768 fresh tasks only after
these objects and the analysis are frozen.  The public frontier executes 1.5B, 7B,
and 32B Qwen2.5-Coder on HumanEval+ and MBPP+ and 1.5B and 7B Qwen2.5 on BBH,
with temperature \(0.6\), top-\(p=0.95\), and at most ten attempts.  The
calibrated simulation compares the pairs shown in
Table~\ref{tab:simulated-first-passage}; it uses seconds for the public panels
and normalized parameter--token slots for SAI-3.
SAI-3 instantiates the one-step case \(L(x)=1\), so
\(q_d^\star(x,z)=q_d(a_1^\star(x)\mid z)\); it does not test
history-dependent tool use.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Controlled confirmation: accurate decisions, rejected exactness}

The SAI-3 confirmation contains 175,104 executions and no censoring
or wrong-shard success.  Regressing held-out mean log first-passage resource on
\(-\log q\) gives an inverse-share exponent of \(0.9868\), with 90\% bootstrap
interval \([0.9784,0.9952]\).  Across the six primary contrasts, the mean
four-term residual is \(0.0100\) nat, its RMS is \(0.0147\) nat, and the 95\%
bootstrap upper bound on RMS is \(0.0350\) nat.  The predicted design agrees
with the held-out resource oracle in all six conditions; the minimum bootstrap
selection probability is \(1.000\), and estimated oracle regret is zero.
For the frozen seven-design, 256-budget-grid family at \(\alpha=0.05\),
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-sample} gives an overall radius of \(0.085\).  Zero
horizon censoring therefore certifies overall 90\% admissibility (lower bound
\(0.915\)).  Moreover,
the archived confirmation was frozen for expected-log closure and does not
retain the clustered CDF bands needed to certify quantile separation.  Thus the
six-of-six agreement is a mechanism result, not a unique proper-time winner
certificate.

Exact closure nevertheless fails its frozen omnibus criterion.  Residuals
increase with one-step tool-policy mismatch with slope \(0.0377\)
(95\% interval \([0.0108,0.0647]\), Holm-adjusted \(p=0.0224\)).  This is about
\(0.0299\) nat, or 3.0\% in multiplicative resource, at the largest tested
mismatch.  A fresh 122,880-execution diagnostic raises repetitions per task
cell from 6 to 32: the mismatch slope falls to \(0.0150\)
(95\% interval \([-0.0098,0.0394]\), adjusted \(p=0.487\)), while the
inverse-share exponent is \(0.9944\) (90\% interval \([0.9865,1.0021]\)).
This attenuation supports finite-repetition bias as a mechanism, but does not
reverse the preregistered rejection.  The defensible result is therefore
\emph{decision-accurate approximate accounting}, not an empirical proof of an
exact universal identity.

\subsection{Observed public frontiers: reliability is often unattained}

The observed ten-retry frontiers provide a necessary negative control.  On
HumanEval+, the best final verified rate is 149/164 for the 7B model,
90.9\% with 95\% Wilson interval \([85.5,94.4]\%\)~\citep{wilson1927probable};
its descriptive empirical
\(T_{90}\) is 736 generated tokens (38.27 seconds), but a finite 95\% interval
is not identified because the lower reliability bound misses the target.  The other HumanEval+
models do not reach 90\%.  The best MBPP+ rate is 323/378 for 32B,
85.4\% \([81.5,88.7]\%\), and the best BBH rate is 951/1,200 for 7B,
79.3\% \([76.9,81.5]\%\).  Their \(T_{90}\) values are therefore censored.
Moreover, even HumanEval+ 7B does not meet a marginal lower-bound admissibility
rule, so it necessarily fails the stronger simultaneous certificate.  The
practical conclusion is not to
declare another model the winner, but to increase the horizon, reduce the
reliability target prospectively, or report that the candidate menu is
inadmissible.

\subsection{Reliability-first rankings are not universal}

Table~\ref{tab:simulated-first-passage} reports the calibrated simulation used
to finalize Figure~\ref{fig:first-passage}.  Positive log-speedup
favours the deployed model.  The 7B candidate reaches 90\% reliability with
16.5\% less normalized resource on SAI-3 and 43.5\% fewer seconds on
HumanEval+, but requires 34.3\% more seconds on MBPP+.  On BBH its simulated
\(T_{90}\) is 65.8\% lower.  Thus neither model size nor one benchmark supplies
a transportable winner.  At the budget where the baseline reaches exactly
90\%, the candidate verifies 93.6\%, 97.9\%, 84.8\%, and 95.9\% of tasks,
respectively.  This fixed-budget contrast is operational: it states how much
reliability a deployment obtains without changing its resource envelope.  All
four paired 95\% intervals for \(\Delta_T\) exclude zero; these
intervals quantify Monte Carlo precision, not uncertainty about executed
routers.

\subsection{What the four terms explain---and what they do not}

Table~\ref{tab:simulated-accounting} applies the complete estimator to the same

standard errors; trial-level standard deviations are \(0.322\), \(1.106\),

inside the exact one-step reference-sequence construction by design.  The
residual shown here instead compares that prediction with end-to-end first
passage under nonzero success of alternative one-step tool choices, and is
therefore a test of transport beyond the reference-sequence regime.

HumanEval+ illustrates the intended positive case: competence and information
more than offset cost and mismatch, and the end-to-end gain remains positive
after a \(-0.163\)-nat residual.  MBPP+ gives the opposite decision: the mean-retry-cost
penalty dominates, so routing information cannot rescue the candidate.  Large
SAI-3 and BBH residuals warn against transporting the exact reference-sequence
identity when alternative tool choices can solve the task.  Most importantly, BBH has
\(\Delta_T=1.074\) but \(\Delta_{\rm obs}=-0.846\): the candidate has a better
90th-percentile completion budget while a worse mean paired log-resource.
This is not a contradiction.  Verified proper time is a population quantile;
the four-term theorem targets an expected log functional.  Crossing
first-passage curves can rank the two objectives differently, which is why
\(\delta\) and the deployment loss must be declared before model selection.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The results support reliability-first selection, not a universal winner.
Freeze the verifier, resource unit, \(B\), and \(\delta\); admit \(d\) only if
a lower confidence bound on \(\Prob(B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}\le B)\) reaches
\(1-\delta\); then
minimize held-out \(\widehat T_d(\delta)\).  Replace the incumbent only when the
simultaneous quantile bands in Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-sample} separate it from
every competitor; otherwise report no winner.

The terms prescribe interventions: negative cost calls for better serving;
negative competence for another backbone; low information for a better signal;
and high mismatch for a better tool policy.  A large residual
invalidates the surrogate and returns the decision to direct measurement.
Proper time selects; four-term accounting audits the mechanism.

The BBH reversal shows that average log resource and SLA-like \(T_{90}\) can
disagree.  The objective must be declared before observing results; the full
curve exposes this tradeoff and permits reranking at another reliability level
without rerunning inference.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The four-dataset accounting is calibrated simulation: it validates estimators,
power, and figure design, not executed-router performance.  Its signal, action
overlap, and BBH cells require held-out randomized runs.  Physical SAI-3 uses a
synthetic benchmark, one dense family, fixed-length slots, and nearly zero
off-reference one-step success; external validity is narrow, and exact closure fails even
there.

HumanEval+ and MBPP+ classes are length strata, not learned solver structures;
BBH lacks a 32B run; and cells below 90\% after ten attempts remain censored,
without finite \(T_{90}\).  The present experiments identify only the one-step
special case of the sequential tool-policy mismatch; history-dependent
evaluation requires a tool benchmark with frozen reference sequences and
held-out instances.

IID retries, reference-sequence success restrictions, and stable retry laws are
approximations:
history, correlation, caching, batching, verifier latency, and adaptive stopping
can change \(t_0\) and \(\overline K\).  Charge signal acquisition
to the same resource ledger.  Verification certifies only the declared criterion, not
usefulness or safety.  If resources cannot be credibly scalarized, report a
Pareto frontier rather than one ranking.
