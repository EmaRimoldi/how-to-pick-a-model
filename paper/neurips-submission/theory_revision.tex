\begin{abstract}
Model selection for verifiable tasks should ask how much resource is required
to obtain a verified solution, not only which model has the highest one-shot
accuracy.  We formalize this objective as a reliability-targeted quantile of
verified first-passage resource, measured in a predeclared unit such as time,
tokens, energy, or money.  Simultaneous finite-sample confidence bands provide
a conservative rule for admitting and comparing a finite menu of deployment
designs, including an explicit no-winner outcome.  To explain measured
differences, we introduce a general action-success process and derive PACT,
Pre-solution Allocation with Constant-cost Trials, as a restricted diagonal
case.  Inside PACT, expected log conditional first-passage resource separates
exactly into relative unit cost, focused competence, mode information, and
allocation mismatch.  Controlled Qwen2.5-Coder experiments recover near-unit
inverse-share scaling and decision-accurate accounting while rejecting exact
closure; public benchmark traces further show that reliability targets are
often unattained.  Thus verified proper time selects the deployment, whereas
the four-term identity audits why that selection succeeds or fails.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Which model should be deployed on a new task whose solution can be checked?
Current practice often evaluates candidate systems end to end and deploys the
one with the best aggregate score.  We call this \emph{benchmark racing}.  It
has produced realistic evaluations, from repository repair against executable
tests to machine-learning engineering against held-out criteria
\citep{jimenez2024swebench,chan2024mlebench}.  Yet aggregate accuracy is
insufficient when models differ in serving cost, can be retried, or have
complementary strengths.  A model that is more accurate per attempt need not
reach the first verified solution with the least time, computation, energy,
tokens, or money.  The same aggregate gain can also arise from cheaper attempts,
greater competence, better pre-solution information, or better allocation.
These mechanisms imply different engineering interventions.

Our conceptual foundation is the proper-time framework of Achille and Soatto
\citep{achille2025agents}.  They study verifiable transductive tasks and argue
that eventual correctness under unbounded search does not characterize useful
intelligence.  Indeed, IID retries with success probability \(p>0\) satisfy
\(\Prob(\text{success by }n)=1-(1-p)^n\to1\).  We therefore require a verified
solution within a predeclared resource horizon and reliability target.  Their
path proper time motivates the principle that information is useful insofar as
it buys speed; our deployment quantity is different: a population quantile of
observed verifier-backed first passage.

Our primary contribution is a finite-sample selection rule for that quantile.
It treats distinct task instances as independent clusters, allows arbitrary
dependence among retries and paired model runs on the same instance, and either
certifies separation or reports no winner.  A general action-success model then
connects routing to the complete first-passage distribution.  Its restricted
PACT case yields the diagnostic identity
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\mathrm{pack}}
={}&\underbrace{\E_S\!\left[\log
  \frac{\kappa(d_0,S)}{\kappa(d,S)}\right]}_{\text{relative unit cost}}
+\underbrace{\E_S\!\left[\log
  \frac{t_0(d_0,S)}{t_0(d,S)}\right]}_{\text{focused competence}}\\[-0.15em]
&+\underbrace{I(S;Z)}_{\text{mode information}}
-\underbrace{\E_Z\KL(\pi_Z\Vert q_Z)}_{\text{allocation mismatch}}.
\label{eq:intro-four-term}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This identity concerns expected log \emph{conditional mean} resource inside
PACT.  It does not decompose the proper-time quantile and is not used to select
a model.  Direct quantile measurement selects; Equation~\eqref{eq:intro-four-term}
explains the mechanism when its assumptions survive held-out diagnostics.

We make three contributions.  First, we define reliability-targeted verified
proper time and give simultaneous finite-sample CDF, quantile, admissibility,
and winner guarantees for a finite model menu and predeclared subpopulations.
Second, we derive the exact first-passage distribution of a routed action bank,
including cross-action success, signal overhead, and unsolved mass.  PACT is a
transparent special case whose four-term identity is explicitly
objective-specific.  Third, we test these restrictions with real language
models.  In 175,104 held-out trajectories over 768 controlled tasks, the
inverse-share exponent is \(0.987\), the four-term residual RMS is \(0.015\)
nat, and model choice agrees with the held-out oracle in all six conditions.
A strict omnibus test rejects exact closure, so the result is
decision-accurate approximate accounting rather than a universal equality.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Classical algorithm selection maps instance features to a solver; empirical
performance models and SATzilla show that runtime-optimal solvers can be
instance dependent~\citep{rice1976algorithm,hutter2014runtime,xu2012satzilla,
kotthoff2016algorithm}.  Racing and best-arm procedures reduce evaluation cost
when identifying a winner~\citep{maron1993hoeffding,maron1997racing,
kaufmann2016bestarm}.  Run2Survive models censored runtime distributions and
risk-sensitive selection~\citep{tornede2020run2survive}, while preferences over
runtime distributions formalize objectives beyond the mean
\citep{graham2023runtime}.  Our target is likewise distributional, but
specialized to verifier-backed first passage and simultaneous quantile
selection.

LLM cascades and routers learn cost--quality decisions over heterogeneous model
pools~\citep{chen2023frugalgpt,ong2024routellm,hu2024routerbench,
dekoninck2025routing}; test-time scaling further shows that sampling effort and
model size interact~\citep{brown2024monkeys,snell2025testtime,wu2025inference,
osca2024}.  We instead freeze the complete routed design and compare its
first-passage distribution.  Finally, the information term in our restricted
identity is the standard reduction in log loss supplied by side information
\citep{jiao2014logloss}.  Our contribution is not a new characterization of
mutual information, but its placement beside measured cost and competence in a
falsifiable first-passage accounting.

\section{Verifier-Backed Model Selection}
\label{sec:problem}

\begin{definition}[Verifiable task family and resource measure]
\label{def:task-family}
A \emph{verifiable task family} is a tuple
\(\mathrm{T}=(\mathrm{X},\mathrm{Y},V,\mu)\).  Each \(x\in\mathrm{X}\) is a
concrete instance for which an artifact \(y\in\mathrm{Y}\) must be constructed.
The distribution \(\mu\) describes future instances, and the predeclared
external verifier \(V:\mathrm{X}\times\mathrm{Y}\to\{0,1\}\) supplies the
common success event.  A \emph{resource measure} assigns every partial
execution a nonnegative additive coordinate \(B\), measured in isolated
GPU-seconds, joules, tokens, dollars, or a predeclared scalarization.
\end{definition}

A \emph{deployment design} \(d\) freezes the backbone and implementation,
prompting and decoding, routing or retry policy, signal acquisition, and
verifier invocation.  Given \(x\), let \(y(B;x)\) be the generally random
artifact available after cumulative resource \(B\).  Probabilities below are
joint over \(x\sim\mu\) and run randomness.

\begin{definition}[Verified first passage]
\label{def:first-passage}
The verified first-passage resource is
\begin{equation}
\tau_d(x):=
\begin{cases}
\inf\{B\ge0:V(x,y(B;x))=1\},&\text{if verification is reached},\\
\infty,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:first-passage}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Reliability-targeted verified proper time]
\label{def:proper-time}
For failure tolerance \(\delta\in(0,1)\), define
\begin{equation}
T_d(\delta):=\inf\{B\ge0:\Prob[\tau_d(x)\le B]\ge1-\delta\}.
\label{eq:proper-time-system}
\end{equation}
This population \((1-\delta)\)-quantile is the smallest resource by which a
verified artifact is produced with the target reliability.  It may be infinite.
``Proper time'' is operational terminology for progress along a frozen design,
not a relativistic invariant; when the resource is not seconds, it should be
read as verified proper resource in the declared unit.
The term \emph{statistically certified} is reserved for the finite-sample bounds
in Section~\ref{sec:finite-sample}.  For finite positive quantiles, define the
proper-time log-speedup
\(\Delta_T(d_0,d;\delta)=\log T_{d_0}(\delta)-\log T_d(\delta)\).
\end{definition}

\begin{problem}[Reliability-first model selection]
\label{prob:model-selection}
Given a finite predeclared menu \(\mathcal D\), common verifier and resource
unit, tolerance \(\delta\), and horizon \(B\), certify which designs satisfy
\(\Prob(\tau_d\le B)\ge1-\delta\), optionally for every declared
subpopulation, and select the admissible design with smallest \(T_d(\delta)\).
If sampling uncertainty does not separate candidates, return no winner.
Different units induce different comparisons; resources that cannot be
credibly scalarized require a Pareto frontier.
\end{problem}

\section{Finite-Sample Certification}
\label{sec:finite-sample}

Let \(\mathcal G\) be predeclared disjoint instance groups with deployment
weights \(w_g\) summing to one.  They may be task modes or operationally
important subpopulations.  For group \(g\), sample independent instances
\(x_{g,1},\ldots,x_{g,n_g}\).  Run each design \(R_g\) times per instance and
record failures as \(\tau=\infty\).  Retries, repeated runs, and paired designs
may be dependent within an instance; independence is required only across
distinct instances.  On a finite budget grid \(\mathcal B\), define
\begin{equation}
U_{d,g,i}(b):=\frac1{R_g}\sum_{r=1}^{R_g}
\mathbf1\{\tau_{d,g,i,r}\le b\},\quad
\widehat F_{d,g}(b):=\frac1{n_g}\sum_iU_{d,g,i}(b),\quad
\widehat F_d(b):=\sum_gw_g\widehat F_{d,g}(b).
\label{eq:cluster-cdf}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Simultaneous first-passage and selection certificate]
\label{thm:finite-sample}
Let \(M=|\mathcal D||\mathcal B|(|\mathcal G|+1)\) and \(\alpha\in(0,1)\).
Set
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_g=\sqrt{\frac{\log(2M/\alpha)}{2n_g}},\qquad
\epsilon=\sqrt{\frac{\log(2M/\alpha)}{2}
                    \sum_g\frac{w_g^2}{n_g}}.
\label{eq:simultaneous-radii}
\end{equation}
With probability at least \(1-\alpha\), simultaneously for every design,
budget, and group,
\begin{equation}
|\widehat F_{d,g}(b)-F_{d,g}(b)|\le\epsilon_g,qquad
|\widehat F_d(b)-F_d(b)|\le\epsilon.
\label{eq:simultaneous-bands}
\end{equation}
Let \(L_d=(\widehat F_d-\epsilon)\vee0\) and
\(U_d=(\widehat F_d+\epsilon)\wedge1\), and define
\begin{equation}
T_d^-:=\inf\{b\in\mathcal B:U_d(b)\ge1-\delta\},\qquad
T_d^+:=\inf\{b\in\mathcal B:L_d(b)\ge1-\delta\}.
\label{eq:quantile-band}
\end{equation}
Then \(T_d^-\le T_d(\delta)\le T_d^+\), up to grid resolution.  A design is
conservatively admissible at horizon \(B\) if \(L_d(B)\ge1-\delta\); replacing
\(L_d\) by every groupwise lower band certifies groupwise reliability.  If an
admissible \(\widehat d\) satisfies
\(T_{\widehat d}^+<\min_{d\ne\widehat d}T_d^-\), it is the unique proper-time
winner over \(\mathcal D\) on the simultaneous event.  Otherwise the
certificate returns no winner.
\end{theorem}

The theorem supplies the statistical meaning of certification.  It is
distribution free but conservative~\citep{hoeffding1963probability}; bootstrap or survival estimators can be
reported descriptively, while winner claims must preserve task-level
clustering and multiplicity.

\section{Action Processes and PACT Accounting}
\label{sec:action-process}

\begin{definition}[Task modes and pre-solution information]
\label{def:packed-objects}
The finite variable \(S\in\mathcal S\) indexes predeclared solver-relevant
subfamilies: \(x\mid S=s\sim\mu_s\), \(\pi(s)=\Prob(S=s)\), and
\(\mu=\sum_s\pi(s)\mu_s\).  A mode represents shared structure that changes
which procedure is effective, not merely a post-hoc label.  A signal
\(Z\in\mathcal Z\) is computed only from information available before a
candidate solution or verifier outcome.  It changes the prior \(\pi\) to
\(\pi_z(s)=\Prob(S=s\mid Z=z)\) and may be informative or independent of \(S\).
Modes, the signal extractor, and their estimation data are frozen before
confirmation.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Frozen action process]
\label{def:action-process}
Fix design \(d\) and backbone \(M_d\).  A finite action bank \(\mathcal A_d\)
contains frozen configurations of that backbone: an action may specify a prompt,
harness, decoding rule, or sequence of inference steps.  Acquiring \(Z=z\)
costs \(c_d(z)\ge0\).  Let
\(\mathcal Q_d=\{q\in[0,1]^{|\mathcal A_d|}:\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_d}q(a)=1\}\)
be the set of allocation distributions.  Given \(z\), the allocator
\(q_z\in\mathcal Q_d\) draws
\(A_k\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\operatorname{Cat}(q_z)\).  A canonical slot is
one complete action execution followed by verification.  On mode \(s\), action
\(a\) succeeds in one slot with probability \(p_d(s,z,a)\).  Conditional on
\((S,Z)\), slots are independent and each costs \(\kappa_d(s)>0\).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Action-success first-passage law]
\label{thm:action-process}
Under Definition~\ref{def:action-process}, let
\begin{equation}
\lambda_d(s,z;q):=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_d}q_z(a)p_d(s,z,a),\qquad
m_d(B;s,z):=\max\!\left\{0,
\left\lfloor\frac{B-c_d(z)}{\kappa_d(s)}\right\rfloor\right\}.
\label{eq:action-hazard}
\end{equation}
The successful slot index \(N_d\mid(s,z)\) is geometric with parameter
\(\lambda_d(s,z;q)\), with \(N_d=\infty\) when \(\lambda_d=0\).  Hence
\begin{equation}
F_{d,q}(B)=\E_{S,Z}\!\left[
1-\{1-\lambda_d(S,Z;q)\}^{m_d(B;S,Z)}
\right].
\label{eq:action-mixture-cdf}
\end{equation}
Consequently, population proper time follows by inserting this CDF into
Definition~\ref{def:proper-time}; it is not an average of modewise quantiles.
Cross-action success, signal overhead, and positive unsolved mass are explicit.
\end{theorem}

Unequal action costs or history-dependent allocations do not obey this simple
geometric law.  They remain valid deployment designs, but their proper time must
be measured directly or modeled as a more general semi-Markov process.

\subsection{PACT as a restricted diagnostic}

PACT abbreviates \emph{Pre-solution Allocation with Constant-cost Trials}.  It
specializes Definition~\ref{def:action-process}: actions are
\(\{a_{d,s}:s\in\mathcal S\}\); signal cost is zero; the matching action succeeds
with \(p_d(s)\in(0,1]\); off-mode actions have zero success; and every slot on
mode \(s\) costs \(\kappa(d,s)>0\).  Mode sufficiency,
\((p_d,\kappa_d)\perp Z\mid S\), prevents the signal from silently changing
competence or cost.  The support condition
\(\pi_z(s)>0\Rightarrow q_z(s)>0\) ensures finite expected resource.  Defining
\(t_0(d,s)=1/p_d(s)\), Theorem~\ref{thm:action-process} gives
\begin{equation}
\E[\tau_{d,q}\mid S=s,Z=z]
=\kappa(d,s)\frac{t_0(d,s)}{q_z(s)}.
\label{eq:packed-law}
\end{equation}

For distributions \(a,b\) on \(\mathcal S\), let
\(H(a)=-\sum_sa(s)\log a(s)\) and
\(\KL(a\Vert b)=\sum_sa(s)\log\{a(s)/b(s)\}\), with standard extended-value
conventions.  Mutual information is
\(I(S;Z)=H(\pi)-\E_ZH(\pi_Z)\).  Define the PACT diagnostic
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L(d,q):=\E_{S,Z}\!\left[
\log\E(\tau_{d,q}\mid S,Z)\right].
\label{eq:packed-risk}
\end{equation}
It is neither \(\E\log\tau\), \(\log\E\tau\), nor \(\log T_d(\delta)\).

\begin{lemma}[Conditional PACT cross-entropy]
\label{lem:conditional}
Under PACT and \(\pi_z\ll q_z\),
\begin{equation}
\E[\log\E(\tau_{d,q}\mid S,z)\mid Z=z]
=\E[\log\{\kappa(d,S)t_0(d,S)\}\mid z]
+H(\pi_z)+\KL(\pi_z\Vert q_z).
\label{eq:conditional-decomposition}
\end{equation}
Thus \(q_z=\pi_z\) uniquely minimizes this conditional expected-log objective.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[PACT four-term expected-log proper-time decomposition]
\label{thm:four-term}
Let designs \(d_0,d\) share \((S,\pi)\), satisfy PACT, and use signals
\(Z_0,Z\) with supported allocations \(q^0,q\).  Define
\begin{align*}
C&:=\E_S\!\left[\log\frac{\kappa(d_0,S)}{\kappa(d,S)}\right],&
\Phi&:=\E_S\!\left[\log\frac{t_0(d_0,S)}{t_0(d,S)}\right],\\
\varepsilon_0&:=\E_{Z_0}\KL(\pi^0_{Z_0}\Vert q^0_{Z_0}),&
\varepsilon&:=\E_Z\KL(\pi_Z\Vert q_Z).
\end{align*}
Then
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L(d_0,q^0)-\mathcal L(d,q)
=C+\Phi+I(S;Z)-I(S;Z_0)+\varepsilon_0-\varepsilon.
\label{eq:four-term-general}
\end{equation}
For an uninformative prior-matched baseline, \(Z_0\perp S\) and
\(q^0=\pi\), so
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\Delta_{\mathrm{pack}}=C+\Phi+I(S;Z)-\varepsilon.}
\label{eq:four-term-baseline}
\end{equation}
These are relative unit cost, focused competence, mode information, and
allocation mismatch.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Objective dependence]
\label{prop:objective-dependence}
Write \(a_s=\kappa(d,s)t_0(d,s)\).  Conditional on \(Z=z\), posterior matching
\(q_z=\pi_z\) minimizes the expected-log PACT objective.  In contrast, the
expected-resource objective \(\sum_s\pi_z(s)a_s/q_z(s)\) is minimized by
\begin{equation}
q_z^\star(s)=
\frac{\sqrt{\pi_z(s)a_s}}{\sum_u\sqrt{\pi_z(u)a_u}}.
\label{eq:sqrt-allocation}
\end{equation}
Thus the allocator depends on the declared deployment loss; posterior matching
is not a universal routing rule.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Testable off-mode departure]
\label{prop:off-mode}
Suppose the matching action has success \(p_s\), while off-mode actions
contribute \(r_{s,z}^{\mathrm{off}}:=\sum_{a\ne a_s}q_z(a)p_d(s,z,a)\).  Ignoring
signal cost, the actual and PACT conditional means have log residual
\begin{equation}
r_d(s,z)=
-\log\!\left(1+\frac{r_{s,z}^{\mathrm{off}}}{q_z(s)p_s}\right).
\label{eq:off-mode-residual}
\end{equation}
If \(r_{s,z}^{\mathrm{off}}/(q_z(s)p_s)\le\xi\), then
\(|r_d(s,z)|\le\log(1+\xi)\).  A positive signal cost instead yields
\(c_d(z)+\kappa_d(s)/\lambda_d(s,z;q)\) and breaks four-term log-additivity.
\end{proposition}

Only the product \(\kappa t_0\) is identified from conditional mean first
passage.  Separating cost from competence requires attempt-level resource logs;
both factors depend on the frozen definition of a slot and are mechanistic, not
causal, without randomized interventions.

\begin{algorithm}[How to Pick the Model]
\label{alg:pick-model}
\begin{tabular}{@{}r p{0.86\linewidth}@{}}
\textit{Input:} &
Verifiable task family, candidate designs \(\mathcal D\), verifier \(V\),
resource unit, tolerance \(\delta\), horizon \(B\), budget grid \(\mathcal B\),
confidence level \(1-\alpha\), and optional groups \(\mathcal G\).\\[0.25em]
\textit{Output:} &
A statistically certified winner or ``no winner,'' plus an optional PACT
mechanism audit.\\[0.35em]
1 & \textit{Freeze the target.} Declare \(V\), the resource unit, \(\delta\),
\(B\), \(\mathcal B\), candidate menu, and groupwise constraints.\\
2 & \textit{Measure first passage.} Stop each run at its first verified artifact
or at \(B\); retain failures as infinite and cluster all paired runs by task.\\
3 & \textit{Construct bands.} Compute Equation~\eqref{eq:cluster-cdf},
simultaneous CDF bands, and \([T_d^-,T_d^+]\) for every design.\\
4 & \textit{Certify or abstain.} Enforce overall and groupwise admissibility.
Name a winner only if its upper proper-time bound lies below every competitor's
lower bound.\\
5 & \textit{Audit the mechanism.} On disjoint calibration and confirmation data,
estimate the action-success matrix, signal cost, and slot cost; test PACT's
diagonal, constant-cost, mode-sufficiency, and inverse-share restrictions.\\
6 & \textit{Explain, do not rerank.} Where PACT is adequate, report the four
terms and held-out residual; keep the direct certificate as selection authority.
\end{tabular}
\end{algorithm}

The chain is explicit: Definition~\ref{def:proper-time} sets the deployment
estimand; Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-sample} selects or abstains;
Theorem~\ref{thm:action-process} models the full curve; and
Theorem~\ref{thm:four-term} supplies a scoped mechanism audit.
