\begin{abstract}
Model selection for verifiable tasks should ask how much resource is required
to obtain a verified solution, not only which model has the highest one-shot
accuracy.  We formalize this objective as a reliability-targeted quantile of
verified first-passage resource, measured in a predeclared unit such as time,
tokens, energy, or money.  Simultaneous finite-sample confidence bands provide
a conservative rule for admitting and comparing a finite menu of deployment
designs, including an explicit no-winner outcome.  To explain measured
differences, we introduce a general tool-trajectory success process and isolate
a restricted canonical-trajectory regime.  In that regime, expected log
conditional first-passage resource separates exactly into relative mean retry
cost, focused competence, mode information, and sequential tool-policy
mismatch.  Controlled Qwen2.5-Coder
experiments recover the one-step special case with near-unit inverse-share
scaling and decision-accurate accounting while rejecting exact closure; public
benchmark traces further show that reliability targets are often unattained.
Thus verified proper time selects the deployment, whereas the four-term
identity audits why that selection succeeds or fails.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Which model should be deployed on a new task whose solution can be checked?
Current practice often evaluates candidate systems end to end and deploys the
one with the best aggregate score.  We call this \emph{benchmark racing}.  It
has produced realistic evaluations, from repository repair against executable
tests to machine-learning engineering against held-out criteria
\citep{jimenez2024swebench,chan2024mlebench}.  Yet aggregate accuracy is
insufficient when models differ in serving cost, can be retried, or have
complementary strengths.  A model that is more accurate per attempt need not
reach the first verified solution with the least time, computation, energy,
tokens, or money.  The same aggregate gain can also arise from cheaper attempts,
greater competence, better pre-solution information, or better allocation.
These mechanisms imply different engineering interventions.

Our conceptual foundation is the proper-time framework of Achille and Soatto
\citep{achille2025agents}.  They study verifiable transductive tasks and argue
that eventual correctness under unbounded search does not characterize useful
intelligence.  Indeed, IID retries with success probability \(p>0\) satisfy
\(\Prob(\text{success by }n)=1-(1-p)^n\to1\).  We therefore require a verified
solution within a predeclared resource horizon and reliability target.  Their
path proper time motivates the principle that information is useful insofar as
it buys speed; our deployment quantity is different: a population quantile of
observed verifier-backed first passage.

Our primary contribution is a finite-sample selection rule for that quantile.
It treats distinct task instances as independent clusters, allows arbitrary
dependence among retries and paired model runs on the same instance, and either
certifies separation or reports no winner.  A general tool-trajectory model then
connects tool selection to first-passage resource.  Its restricted
canonical-trajectory regime yields the diagnostic identity against an
uninformative, reference-prior-matched baseline,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\mathrm{traj}}
={}&\underbrace{\E_{S,Z_0}\log\overline K_{d_0}(S,Z_0)
-\E_{S,Z}\log\overline K_d(S,Z)}_{\text{relative mean retry cost}}
+\underbrace{\E_S\!\left[\log
  \frac{t_0(d_0,S)}{t_0(d,S)}\right]}_{\text{focused competence}}\\[-0.15em]
&+\underbrace{I(S;Z)}_{\text{mode information}}
-\underbrace{\E_Z\KL(\rho_Z\Vert Q_{d,Z})}_{\text{tool-trajectory mismatch}}.
\label{eq:intro-four-term}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This identity concerns expected log \emph{conditional mean} resource inside
this regime.  It does not decompose the proper-time quantile and is not used to select
a model.  Direct quantile measurement selects; Equation~\eqref{eq:intro-four-term}
explains the mechanism when its assumptions survive held-out diagnostics.

We make three contributions.  First, we define reliability-targeted verified
proper time and give simultaneous finite-sample CDF, quantile, admissibility,
and winner guarantees for a finite model menu and predeclared subpopulations.
Second, we derive the exact mean first-passage resource of a sequential tool
policy under complete IID retries.  A transparent canonical-trajectory
specialization has a KL mismatch over tool trajectories that decomposes into
per-step tool-call divergences.  Third, we test the one-step restriction with
real language models.  In 175,104 held-out trajectories over 768 controlled
tasks, the inverse-share exponent is \(0.987\), the four-term residual RMS is
\(0.015\) nat, and model choice agrees with the held-out oracle in all six conditions.
A strict omnibus test rejects exact closure, so the result is
decision-accurate approximate accounting rather than a universal equality.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Classical algorithm selection maps instance features to a solver; empirical
performance models and SATzilla show that runtime-optimal solvers can be
instance dependent~\citep{rice1976algorithm,hutter2014runtime,xu2012satzilla,
kotthoff2016algorithm}.  Racing and best-arm procedures reduce evaluation cost
when identifying a winner~\citep{maron1993hoeffding,maron1997racing,
kaufmann2016bestarm}.  Run2Survive models censored runtime distributions and
risk-sensitive selection~\citep{tornede2020run2survive}, while preferences over
runtime distributions formalize objectives beyond the mean
\citep{graham2023runtime}.  Our target is likewise distributional, but
specialized to verifier-backed first passage and simultaneous quantile
selection.

LLM cascades and routers learn cost--quality decisions over heterogeneous model
pools~\citep{chen2023frugalgpt,ong2024routellm,hu2024routerbench,
dekoninck2025routing}; test-time scaling further shows that sampling effort and
model size interact~\citep{brown2024monkeys,snell2025testtime,wu2025inference,
osca2024}.  We instead freeze the complete routed design and compare its
first-passage distribution.  Finally, the information term in our restricted
identity is the standard reduction in log loss supplied by side information
\citep{jiao2014logloss}.  Our contribution is not a new characterization of
mutual information, but its placement beside measured cost and competence in a
falsifiable first-passage accounting.

\section{Verifier-Backed Model Selection}
\label{sec:problem}

\begin{definition}[Verifiable task family and resource measure]
\label{def:task-family}
A \emph{verifiable task family} is a tuple
\(\mathrm{T}=(\mathrm{X},\mathrm{Y},V,\mu)\).  Each \(x\in\mathrm{X}\) is a
concrete instance for which an artifact \(y\in\mathrm{Y}\) must be constructed.
The distribution \(\mu\) describes future instances, and the predeclared
external verifier \(V:\mathrm{X}\times\mathrm{Y}\to\{0,1\}\) supplies the
common success event.  A \emph{resource measure} assigns every partial
execution a nonnegative additive coordinate \(B\), measured in isolated
GPU-seconds, joules, tokens, dollars, or a predeclared scalarization.
\end{definition}

A \emph{deployment design} \(d\) freezes the backbone and implementation,
prompting and decoding, routing or retry policy, signal acquisition, and
verifier invocation.  Given \(x\), let \(y(B;x)\) be the generally random
artifact available after cumulative resource \(B\).  Probabilities below are
joint over \(x\sim\mu\) and run randomness.

\begin{definition}[Verified first passage]
\label{def:first-passage}
The verified first-passage resource is
\begin{equation}
B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(x):=
\begin{cases}
\inf\{B\ge0:V(x,y(B;x))=1\},&\text{if verification is reached},\\
\infty,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:first-passage}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Reliability-targeted verified proper time]
\label{def:proper-time}
For failure tolerance \(\delta\in(0,1)\), define
\begin{equation}
T_d(\delta):=\inf\{B\ge0:\Prob[B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(x)\le B]\ge1-\delta\}.
\label{eq:proper-time-system}
\end{equation}
This population \((1-\delta)\)-quantile is the smallest resource by which a
verified artifact is produced with the target reliability.  It may be infinite.
``Proper time'' is operational terminology for progress along a frozen design,
not a relativistic invariant; when the resource is not seconds, it should be
read as verified proper resource in the declared unit.
The term \emph{statistically certified} is reserved for the finite-sample bounds
in Section~\ref{sec:finite-sample}.  For finite positive quantiles, define the
proper-time log-speedup
\(\Delta_T(d_0,d;\delta)=\log T_{d_0}(\delta)-\log T_d(\delta)\).
\end{definition}

\begin{problem}[Reliability-first model selection]
\label{prob:model-selection}
Given a finite predeclared menu \(\mathcal D\), common verifier and resource
unit, tolerance \(\delta\), and horizon \(B\), certify which designs satisfy
\(\Prob(B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}\le B)\ge1-\delta\), optionally for every declared
subpopulation, and select the admissible design with smallest \(T_d(\delta)\).
If sampling uncertainty does not separate candidates, return no winner.
Different units induce different comparisons; resources that cannot be
credibly scalarized require a Pareto frontier.
\end{problem}

\section{Finite-Sample Certification}
\label{sec:finite-sample}

Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\) be independent task instances. For each design \(d\), instance \(x_i\), and retrial \(r\in\{1,\ldots,R\}\),
let \(B_{ir}^d\) be the verified first-passage resource.

For a finite budget grid \(\mathcal B\), define
\begin{equation}
  U_i^d(b)
  :=
  \frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=1}^R
  \mathbf 1\{B_{ir}^d\le b\},
  \qquad
  \widehat F_g^d(b)
  :=
  \frac{1}{n_g}\sum_{i\in I_g}U_i^d(b),
  \qquad
  \widehat F^d(b)
  :=
  \sum_{g\in\mathcal G}w_g\widehat F_g^d(b).
  \label{eq:cluster-cdf}
\end{equation}
Here \(U_i^d(b)\) is the fraction of repeated runs that solve instance \(i\)
within budget \(b\), \(\widehat F_g^d\) is the empirical first-passage CDF
within group \(g\), and \(\widehat F^d\) is the deployment-weighted CDF.  Write
\(F_g^d(b):=\E[\widehat F_g^d(b)]\) and
\(F^d(b):=\sum_{g\in\mathcal G}w_gF_g^d(b)\) for their population targets.

\begin{theorem}[Simultaneous first-passage and selection certificate]
\label{thm:finite-sample}
Let \(M:=|\mathcal D|\,|\mathcal B|\,(|\mathcal G|+1)\) and
\(\alpha\in(0,1)\).  Set
\begin{equation}
  \epsilon_g
  :=\sqrt{\frac{\log(2M/\alpha)}{2n_g}},
  \qquad
  \epsilon
  :=\sqrt{\frac{\log(2M/\alpha)}{2}
                    \sum_{g\in\mathcal G}\frac{w_g^2}{n_g}}.
\label{eq:simultaneous-radii}
\end{equation}
With probability at least \(1-\alpha\), simultaneously for every
\(d\in\mathcal D\), \(b\in\mathcal B\), and \(g\in\mathcal G\),
\begin{equation}
  \left|\widehat F_g^d(b)-F_g^d(b)\right|\le\epsilon_g,
  \qquad
  \left|\widehat F^d(b)-F^d(b)\right|\le\epsilon.
\label{eq:simultaneous-bands}
\end{equation}
Let
\[
  L^d(b):=\max\{0,\widehat F^d(b)-\epsilon\},
  \qquad
  U^d(b):=\min\{1,\widehat F^d(b)+\epsilon\},
\]
and define
\begin{equation}
  T_d^-:=\inf\{b\in\mathcal B:U^d(b)\ge1-\delta\},
  \qquad
  T_d^+:=\inf\{b\in\mathcal B:L^d(b)\ge1-\delta\}.
\label{eq:quantile-band}
\end{equation}
Then \(T_d^-\le T_d(\delta)\le T_d^+\), up to grid resolution.  A design is
conservatively admissible at horizon \(B\) if \(L^d(B)\ge1-\delta\).  Requiring
\(\max\{0,\widehat F_g^d(B)-\epsilon_g\}\ge1-\delta\) for every group certifies
groupwise reliability.  If an
admissible \(\widehat d\) satisfies
\(T_{\widehat d}^+<\min_{d\ne\widehat d}T_d^-\), it is the unique proper-time
winner over \(\mathcal D\) on the simultaneous event.  Otherwise the
certificate returns no winner.
\end{theorem}

The theorem supplies the statistical meaning of certification.  It is
distribution free but conservative~\citep{hoeffding1963probability}; bootstrap or survival estimators can be
reported descriptively, while winner claims must preserve task-level
clustering and multiplicity.

\section{Tool Trajectories and Four-Term Accounting}
\label{sec:action-process}

\begin{definition}[Latent task modes and canonical tool trajectories]
\label{def:canonical-objects}
Let \(X\sim\mu\) be a task instance.  The finite latent variable
\(S\in\mathcal S\) groups instances that share solver-relevant structural
regularities.  In particular, instances in the same mode can be addressed by
the same canonical tool trajectory.  For every \(s\in\mathcal S\), fix one
canonical trajectory \(\tau_s^\star\).  If two proposed groups have the same
canonical trajectory, they are treated as one mode in this analysis.

Let \(\pi(s)=\Prob(S=s)\).  An optional pre-solution signal
\(Z\in\mathcal Z\), available before the first tool call, induces the posterior
\(\pi_z(s)=\Prob(S=s\mid Z=z)\).  If no signal is used, \(Z\) is constant and
\(\pi_z=\pi\).  Define the corresponding reference-trajectory distribution by
\begin{equation}
\rho_z(\tau)
:=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\pi_z(s)
\mathbf 1\{\tau=\tau_s^\star\}.
\label{eq:reference-trajectory-law}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, \(\rho_z(\tau_s^\star)=\pi_z(s)\).  Thus \(\rho_z\) is the
posterior over the canonical trajectories after observing \(z\): it describes
which reference trajectory is appropriate for the current instance, not which
trajectory the model generates.  Modes, canonical trajectories, and the signal
extractor are frozen before confirmation.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Sequential tool policy and trajectory]
\label{def:action-process}
Fix a backbone model \(M\), a deployment design \(d\) built on \(M\), and a
finite tool set \(\mathcal A\). Consider one complete attempt, suppressing the
retry index. Before the \(k\)-th tool selection within that attempt, the history
\(H_k\) contains the task instance, previously selected tools, and their outputs.
Given the pre-solution signal \(Z\), the next tool identity satisfies
\begin{equation}
A_k\sim q_d(\cdot\mid Z,H_k),
\qquad A_k\in\mathcal A.
\label{eq:tool-policy}
\end{equation}
Let \(L\) be the number of tool calls made before the design terminates.
A realization
\[
\tau=(a_1,\ldots,a_L)
\]
is a \emph{tool trajectory}: the ordered sequence of tool identities selected
during one complete attempt. The policy, interacting with the fixed tool set,
induces a distribution \(Q_d(\cdot\mid x,z)\) over variable-length tool
trajectories.  If termination is a deterministic rule in design \(d\), its
probability along deterministic tool outputs is
\begin{equation}
Q_d(\tau\mid x,z)
=
\prod_{k=1}^{L}q_d(a_k\mid z,h_k).
\label{eq:trajectory-policy}
\end{equation}
Interpretation of tool outputs and final-answer generation remain part of
design \(d\).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Variable-cost retry identity]
\label{prop:retry-first-passage}
Fix an instance \(x\) and, when available, a realized pre-solution signal \(z\).
For retry \(r\), let \(Y_r\) be the resulting artifact and \(K_{d,r}>0\) its
realized resource cost.  Assume that, conditional on \((x,z)\), the pairs
\(\bigl(K_{d,r},V(x,Y_r)\bigr)\) are IID across complete retries.

Define
\begin{equation}
\lambda_d(x,z)
:=
\Prob\!\left(V(x,Y_r)=1\mid x,z\right)
=
\sum_\tau Q_d(\tau\mid x,z)
\Prob\!\left(V(x,Y_r)=1\mid x,z,\tau\right).
\label{eq:per-retry-success}
\end{equation}
Thus, \(\lambda_d(x,z)\) is the probability that one complete retry produces
a verified solution.

Define
\begin{equation}
\overline K_d(x,z)
:=
\E[K_{d,r}\mid x,z]
=
\sum_\tau Q_d(\tau\mid x,z)
\E[K_{d,r}\mid x,z,\tau].
\label{eq:mean-retry-cost}
\end{equation}
Thus, \(\overline K_d(x,z)\) is the expected resource cost of one complete
retry, averaged over the tool trajectories generated by the policy.

Let
\begin{equation}
R_d(x,z)
:=
\inf\{r\ge1:V(x,Y_r)=1\}
\end{equation}
be the index of the first retry that produces a verified solution. Then
\begin{equation}
B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(x)
=
c_d(z)+\sum_{r=1}^{R_d(x,z)}K_{d,r},
\end{equation}
and, whenever \(\lambda_d(x,z)>0\),
\begin{equation}
\E[B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(x)\mid x,z]
=
c_d(z)
+
\frac{\overline K_d(x,z)}{\lambda_d(x,z)}.
\label{eq:variable-cost-first-passage}
\end{equation}
Here \(c_d(z)\ge0\) is only the incremental cost of acquiring the signal.  Set
\(c_d(z)=0\) when the signal is supplied with the task or its cost is already
included in \(K_{d,r}\).  If no signal is used, omit \(z\) throughout.
\end{proposition}


\subsection{Canonical tool-trajectory regime}

To obtain a four-term identity, consider the following scoped regime.  After
observing \(z\), design \(d\) induces a supported trajectory law \(Q_{d,z}\),
with \(\rho_z\ll Q_{d,z}\).  This restriction requires the policy's trajectory
law, conditional on the pre-solution signal, to be common across instances;
the task affects success through its mode and canonical trajectory.  Conditional
on \((S=s,Z=z)\), complete retries are IID and their success and cost laws are
common to the instances in that mode.  For an instance of mode \(s\), assume
\begin{equation}
\Prob\!\left(V(X,Y_r)=1\mid
S=s,Z=z,\boldsymbol{\tau}_r=\tau\right)
=p_d(s)\mathbf 1\{\tau=\tau_s^\star\},
\qquad p_d(s)>0.
\label{eq:canonical-success}
\end{equation}
Thus the canonical trajectory succeeds with probability \(p_d(s)\), while
alternative trajectories have zero success in the exact regime.  The verifier
may accept alternatives in the general deployment; Proposition~\ref{prop:off-reference}
quantifies the resulting departure.  Let \(\overline K_d(s,z)\) be the mean
retry cost from Proposition~\ref{prop:retry-first-passage}, conditional on
\((S=s,Z=z)\), and define \(t_0(d,s)=1/p_d(s)\).

\begin{proposition}[Canonical-trajectory first-passage identity]
\label{prop:canonical-first-passage}
In the canonical tool-trajectory regime,
\begin{equation}
\lambda_d(s,z)=Q_{d,z}(\tau_s^\star)p_d(s).
\label{eq:canonical-success-probability}
\end{equation}
If the signal has no separate incremental cost, or that cost is included in the
retry ledger, then
\begin{equation}
\E[B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}\mid S=s,Z=z]
=\overline K_d(s,z)
\frac{t_0(d,s)}{Q_{d,z}(\tau_s^\star)}.
\label{eq:canonical-law}
\end{equation}
With a separate one-time signal cost, \(c_d(z)\) is added to the right-hand
side and the log-additive decomposition below is no longer exact.
\end{proposition}

The previous controlled allocation model is the one-step special case
\(\tau_s^\star=(a_{d,s})\) and
\(Q_{d,z}(\tau_s^\star)=q_z(s)\).

For distributions \(a,b\) on the tool-trajectory space, let
\(H(a)=-\sum_\tau a(\tau)\log a(\tau)\) and
\(\KL(a\Vert b)=\sum_\tau a(\tau)\log\{a(\tau)/b(\tau)\}\), with standard
extended-value conventions.  Mutual information remains
\(I(S;Z)=H(\pi)-\E_ZH(\pi_Z)\).  Define the canonical-regime diagnostic
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L(d):=\E_{S,Z}\!\left[
\log\E(B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}\mid S,Z)\right].
\label{eq:canonical-risk}
\end{equation}
It is neither \(\E\log B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}\),
\(\log\E B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}\), nor \(\log T_d(\delta)\).

\begin{lemma}[Conditional tool-trajectory cross-entropy]
\label{lem:conditional}
Under the canonical tool-trajectory regime and \(\rho_z\ll Q_{d,z}\),
\begin{equation}
\E[\log\E(B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}\mid S,z)\mid Z=z]
=\E[\log\{\overline K_d(S,z)t_0(d,S)\}\mid z]
+H(\rho_z)+\KL(\rho_z\Vert Q_{d,z}).
\label{eq:conditional-decomposition}
\end{equation}
The KL term is minimized by \(Q_{d,z}=\rho_z\) when the mean-cost term is held
fixed.  With trajectory-dependent costs, changing \(Q_{d,z}\) generally changes
both \(\overline K_d(S,z)\) and the KL term.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Sequential tool-call form of the mismatch]
\label{lem:sequential-kl}
Under the canonical regime, write \(A_{<k}=(A_1,\ldots,A_{k-1})\) for the sampled
tool-identity prefix.  Factoring the reference and model trajectory laws over
these prefixes gives
\begin{equation}
\KL(\rho_z\Vert Q_{d,z})
=\E_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\sim\rho_z}\!\left[
\sum_{k=1}^{L}
\KL\!\left(\rho(\cdot\mid z,A_{<k})\Vert
q_d(\cdot\mid z,A_{<k})\right)\right],
\label{eq:sequential-kl}
\end{equation}
where \(q_d(\cdot\mid z,A_{<k})\) is the next-tool policy induced by the
trajectory law.  For a single deterministic reference trajectory, the contribution along its
tool-identity prefixes is
\(-\sum_{k=1}^{L}\log q_d(a_k^\star\mid z,a_{<k}^\star)\).  Thus the trajectory
KL is operationally a sum of next-tool mismatches, not a divergence between
mode frequencies.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Four-term expected-log first-passage decomposition]
\label{thm:four-term}
Let designs \(d_0,d\) share \((S,\pi)\) and the canonical map
\(s\mapsto\tau_s^\star\), satisfy the canonical tool-trajectory regime, and use
signals \(Z_0,Z\) with supported trajectory laws \(Q^0,Q\).  Assume separate
signal costs are zero or included in the retry ledgers.  Define
\begin{align*}
C&:=\E_{S,Z_0}\log\overline K_{d_0}(S,Z_0)
-\E_{S,Z}\log\overline K_d(S,Z),&
\Phi&:=\E_S\!\left[\log\frac{t_0(d_0,S)}{t_0(d,S)}\right],\\
\varepsilon_0&:=\E_{Z_0}\KL(\rho^0_{Z_0}\Vert Q^0_{d_0,Z_0}),&
\varepsilon&:=\E_Z\KL(\rho_Z\Vert Q_{d,Z}).
\end{align*}
Then
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L(d_0)-\mathcal L(d)
=C+\Phi+I(S;Z)-I(S;Z_0)+\varepsilon_0-\varepsilon.
\label{eq:four-term-general}
\end{equation}
For an uninformative reference-prior-matched baseline, \(Z_0\perp S\) and
\(Q^0_{d_0,Z_0}=\rho^0_{Z_0}\), so
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\Delta_{\mathrm{traj}}=C+\Phi+I(S;Z)-\varepsilon.}
\label{eq:four-term-baseline}
\end{equation}
The terms are relative mean retry cost, focused competence, mode information,
and sequential tool-trajectory mismatch.  Because trajectory costs vary,
\(C\) depends on the policy-induced trajectory law; the decomposition is
mechanistic rather than a causal separation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Cost--policy coupling]
\label{prop:objective-dependence}
Let
\begin{equation}
k_d(s,z,\tau):=\E[K_{d,r}\mid S=s,Z=z,\boldsymbol\tau_r=\tau].
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\overline K_d(s,z)=\sum_\tau Q_{d,z}(\tau)k_d(s,z,\tau).
\label{eq:cost-policy-coupling}
\end{equation}
Consequently, changing the trajectory policy generally changes both the mean
retry-cost term and the trajectory-mismatch term.  Reference matching minimizes
the KL contribution only when the mean-cost term is held fixed.  If
\(k_d(s,z,\tau)\) is constant in \(\tau\), the cost term is policy invariant.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Testable off-reference departure]
\label{prop:off-reference}
Suppose the canonical trajectory has success \(p_s\), while alternative
trajectories contribute
\(r_{s,z}^{\mathrm{alt}}:=\sum_{\tau\ne\tau_s^\star}
Q_{d,z}(\tau)\Prob(V(X,Y_r)=1\mid
S=s,Z=z,\boldsymbol\tau_r=\tau)\).  Ignoring signal cost, the actual and
canonical-regime conditional means have log residual
\begin{equation}
r_d(s,z)=
-\log\!\left(1+\frac{r_{s,z}^{\mathrm{alt}}}
{Q_{d,z}(\tau_s^\star)p_s}\right).
\label{eq:off-reference-residual}
\end{equation}
If \(r_{s,z}^{\mathrm{alt}}/
\{Q_{d,z}(\tau_s^\star)p_s\}\le\xi\), then
\(|r_d(s,z)|\le\log(1+\xi)\).  A positive signal cost instead yields
\(c_d(z)+\overline K_d(s,z)/\lambda_d(s,z)\) and breaks four-term
log-additivity.
The verifier still accepts every valid alternative trajectory; the proposition
only quantifies when the canonical-trajectory diagnostic ceases to be exact.
\end{proposition}

Separating \(\overline K_d\) from \(t_0\) requires retry-level resource and
verification logs.  Because \(\overline K_d\) is averaged under \(Q_{d,z}\), a
policy change can alter both cost and mismatch.  The four terms are therefore a
mechanistic accounting, not a causal separation without randomized
interventions.

\begin{algorithm}[How to Pick the Model]
\label{alg:pick-model}
\begin{tabular}{@{}r p{0.86\linewidth}@{}}
\textit{Input:} &
Verifiable task family, candidate designs \(\mathcal D\), verifier \(V\),
resource unit, tolerance \(\delta\), horizon \(B\), budget grid \(\mathcal B\),
confidence level \(1-\alpha\), and optional groups \(\mathcal G\).\\[0.25em]
\textit{Output:} &
A statistically certified winner or ``no winner,'' plus an optional
canonical-trajectory mechanism audit.\\[0.35em]
1 & \textit{Freeze the target.} Declare \(V\), the resource unit, \(\delta\),
\(B\), \(\mathcal B\), candidate menu, and groupwise constraints.\\
2 & \textit{Measure first passage.} Stop each run at its first verified artifact
or at \(B\); retain failures as infinite and cluster all paired runs by task.\\
3 & \textit{Construct bands.} Compute Equation~\eqref{eq:cluster-cdf},
simultaneous CDF bands, and \([T_d^-,T_d^+]\) for every design.\\
4 & \textit{Certify or abstain.} Enforce overall and groupwise admissibility.
Name a winner only if its upper proper-time bound lies below every competitor's
lower bound.\\
5 & \textit{Audit the mechanism.} On disjoint calibration and confirmation data,
estimate canonical-trajectory probabilities, conditional success, variable
retry costs, and any incremental signal cost; test IID retries, the common
trajectory law, canonical success, and off-reference success.\\
6 & \textit{Explain, do not rerank.} Where the canonical regime is adequate, report the four
terms, per-step tool-call KL, and held-out residual; keep the direct certificate
as selection authority.
\end{tabular}
\end{algorithm}

The chain is explicit: Definition~\ref{def:proper-time} sets the deployment
estimand; Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-sample} selects or abstains;
Proposition~\ref{prop:retry-first-passage} links variable retry costs to mean
first passage; and
Theorem~\ref{thm:four-term} supplies a scoped tool-policy mechanism audit.
