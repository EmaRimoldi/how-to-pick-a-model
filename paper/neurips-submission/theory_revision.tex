\begin{abstract}
Model selection for verifiable tasks should ask how much resource is required
to obtain a verified solution, not only which model has the highest one-shot
accuracy.  We formalize this objective as a reliability-targeted quantile of
verified first-passage resource, measured in a predeclared unit such as time,
tokens, energy, or money.  Simultaneous finite-sample confidence bands provide
a conservative rule for admitting and comparing a finite menu of deployment
designs, including an explicit no-winner outcome.  To explain measured
differences, we introduce a sequential tool-allocation process and isolate
a restricted reference-sequence regime.  In that regime, expected log
conditional first-passage resource separates exactly into relative mean retry
cost, focused competence, signal information about appropriate tool use, and
sequential tool-policy mismatch.  Controlled Qwen2.5-Coder
experiments recover the one-step special case with near-unit inverse-share
scaling and decision-accurate accounting while rejecting exact closure; public
benchmark traces further show that reliability targets are often unattained.
Thus verified proper time selects the deployment, whereas the four-term
identity audits why that selection succeeds or fails.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Which model should be deployed on a new task whose solution can be checked?
Current practice often evaluates candidate systems end to end and deploys the
one with the best aggregate score.  We call this \emph{benchmark racing}.  It
has produced realistic evaluations, from repository repair against executable
tests to machine-learning engineering against held-out criteria
\citep{jimenez2024swebench,chan2024mlebench}.  Yet aggregate accuracy is
insufficient when models differ in serving cost, can be retried, or have
complementary strengths.  A model that is more accurate per attempt need not
reach the first verified solution with the least time, computation, energy,
tokens, or money.  The same aggregate gain can also arise from cheaper attempts,
greater competence, better pre-solution information, or better allocation.
These mechanisms imply different engineering interventions.

Our conceptual foundation is the proper-time framework of Achille and Soatto
\citep{achille2025agents}.  They study verifiable transductive tasks and argue
that eventual correctness under unbounded search does not characterize useful
intelligence.  Indeed, IID retries with success probability \(p>0\) satisfy
\(\Prob(\text{success by }n)=1-(1-p)^n\to1\).  We therefore require a verified
solution within a predeclared resource horizon and reliability target.  Their
path proper time motivates the principle that information is useful insofar as
it buys speed; our deployment quantity is different: a population quantile of
observed verifier-backed first passage.

Our primary contribution is a finite-sample selection rule for that quantile.
It treats distinct task instances as independent clusters, allows arbitrary
dependence among retries and paired model runs on the same instance, and either
certifies separation or reports no winner.  A sequential tool-allocation model
then connects tool selection to first-passage resource.  Its restricted
reference-sequence regime yields the diagnostic identity against an
uninformative, reference-prior-matched baseline,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\mathrm{tool}}
={}&\underbrace{\E_{X,Z_0}\log\overline K_{d_0}(X,Z_0)
-\E_{X,Z}\log\overline K_d(X,Z)}_{\text{relative mean retry cost}}
+\underbrace{\E_X\!\left[\log
  \frac{t_0(d_0,X)}{t_0(d,X)}\right]}_{\text{focused competence}}\\[-0.15em]
&+\underbrace{G_Z}_{\text{tool information}}
-\underbrace{\varepsilon_d}_{\text{tool-policy mismatch}}.
\label{eq:intro-four-term}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This identity concerns expected log \emph{conditional mean} resource inside
this regime.  It does not decompose the proper-time quantile and is not used to select
a model.  Direct quantile measurement selects; Equation~\eqref{eq:intro-four-term}
explains the mechanism when its assumptions survive held-out diagnostics.

We make three contributions.  First, we define reliability-targeted verified
proper time and give simultaneous finite-sample CDF, quantile, admissibility,
and winner guarantees for a finite model menu.
Second, we derive the exact mean first-passage resource of a sequential tool
policy under complete IID retries.  A transparent reference-sequence
specialization yields a mismatch that is a sum of per-decision tool-policy
divergences.  Third, we test the one-step restriction with
real language models.  In 175,104 held-out executions over 768 controlled
tasks, the inverse-share exponent is \(0.987\), the four-term residual RMS is
\(0.015\) nat, and model choice agrees with the held-out oracle in all six conditions.
A strict omnibus test rejects exact closure, so the result is
decision-accurate approximate accounting rather than a universal equality.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Classical algorithm selection maps instance features to a solver; empirical
performance models and SATzilla show that runtime-optimal solvers can be
instance dependent~\citep{rice1976algorithm,hutter2014runtime,xu2012satzilla,
kotthoff2016algorithm}.  Racing and best-arm procedures reduce evaluation cost
when identifying a winner~\citep{maron1993hoeffding,maron1997racing,
kaufmann2016bestarm}.  Run2Survive models censored runtime distributions and
risk-sensitive selection~\citep{tornede2020run2survive}, while preferences over
runtime distributions formalize objectives beyond the mean
\citep{graham2023runtime}.  Our target is likewise distributional, but
specialized to verifier-backed first passage and simultaneous quantile
selection.

LLM cascades and routers learn cost--quality decisions over heterogeneous model
pools~\citep{chen2023frugalgpt,ong2024routellm,hu2024routerbench,
dekoninck2025routing}; test-time scaling further shows that sampling effort and
model size interact~\citep{brown2024monkeys,snell2025testtime,wu2025inference,
osca2024}.  We instead freeze the complete routed design and compare its
first-passage distribution.  Finally, the information term in our restricted
identity is the standard reduction in log loss supplied by side information
\citep{jiao2014logloss}.  Our contribution is not a new characterization of
mutual information, but its placement beside measured cost and competence in a
falsifiable first-passage accounting.

\section{Verifier-Backed Model Selection}
\label{sec:problem}

\begin{definition}[Verifiable task family and resource measure]
\label{def:task-family}
A \emph{verifiable task family} is a tuple
\(\mathrm{T}=(\mathrm{X},\mathrm{Y},V,\mu)\).  Each \(x\in\mathrm{X}\) is a
concrete instance for which an artifact \(y\in\mathrm{Y}\) must be constructed.
The distribution \(\mu\) describes future instances, and the predeclared
external verifier \(V:\mathrm{X}\times\mathrm{Y}\to\{0,1\}\) supplies the
common success event.  A \emph{resource measure} assigns every partial
execution a nonnegative additive coordinate \(B\), measured in isolated
GPU-seconds, joules, tokens, dollars, or a predeclared scalarization.
\end{definition}

A \emph{deployment design} \(d\) freezes the backbone and implementation,
prompting and decoding, routing or retry policy, signal acquisition, and
verifier invocation.  Given \(x\), let \(y(B;x)\) be the generally random
artifact available after cumulative resource \(B\).  Probabilities below are
joint over \(x\sim\mu\) and run randomness.

\begin{definition}[Verified first passage]
\label{def:first-passage}
The verified first-passage resource is
\begin{equation}
B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(x):=
\begin{cases}
\inf\{B\ge0:V(x,y(B;x))=1\},&\text{if verification is reached},\\
\infty,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:first-passage}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Reliability-targeted verified proper time]
\label{def:proper-time}
For failure tolerance \(\delta\in(0,1)\), define
\begin{equation}
T_d(\delta):=\inf\{B\ge0:\Prob[B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(x)\le B]\ge1-\delta\}.
\label{eq:proper-time-system}
\end{equation}
This population \((1-\delta)\)-quantile is the smallest resource by which a
verified artifact is produced with the target reliability.  It may be infinite.
``Proper time'' is operational terminology for progress along a frozen design,
not a relativistic invariant; when the resource is not seconds, it should be
read as verified proper resource in the declared unit.
The term \emph{statistically certified} is reserved for the finite-sample bounds
in Section~\ref{sec:finite-sample}.  For finite positive quantiles, define the
proper-time log-speedup
\(\Delta_T(d_0,d;\delta)=\log T_{d_0}(\delta)-\log T_d(\delta)\).
\end{definition}

\begin{problem}[Reliability-first model selection]
\label{prob:model-selection}
Given a finite predeclared menu \(\mathcal D\), common verifier and resource
unit, tolerance \(\delta\), and horizon \(B\), certify which designs satisfy
\(\Prob(B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}\le B)\ge1-\delta\), and select the admissible design
with smallest \(T_d(\delta)\).
If sampling uncertainty does not separate candidates, return no winner.
Different units induce different comparisons; resources that cannot be
credibly scalarized require a Pareto frontier.
\end{problem}

\section{Finite-Sample Certification}
\label{sec:finite-sample}

The proper time in Definition~\ref{def:proper-time} is a population quantity,
but an experiment observes only finitely many task instances.  We therefore
need to determine whether the observed first-passage curves are sufficiently
precise to certify a reliability target and distinguish candidate designs.

Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\mu\) be independent task
instances.  For every design \(d\in\mathcal D\), instance \(x_i\), and retry
\(r\in\{1,\ldots,R\}\), let \(B_{ir}^d\in[0,\infty]\) denote the verified
first-passage resource.  Runs that do not produce a verified artifact within
the experimental horizon are recorded as \(B_{ir}^d=\infty\).

For a finite predeclared budget grid \(\mathcal B\), define
\begin{equation}
U_i^d(b)
:=
\frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=1}^{R}
\mathbf 1\{B_{ir}^d\le b\},
\qquad
\widehat F_d(b)
:=
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}U_i^d(b).
\label{eq:cluster-cdf}
\end{equation}
Here \(U_i^d(b)\) is the fraction of retries that solve instance \(x_i\)
within budget \(b\), while \(\widehat F_d(b)\) estimates the population
first-passage CDF
\[
F_d(b):=\Pr(B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}\le b).
\]
Retries and paired design runs may be arbitrarily dependent within the same
instance.  The independent statistical units are the \(n\) task instances,
not the \(nR\) executions.


Fix a confidence level \(1-\alpha\).  Define the simultaneous uncertainty
radius
\begin{equation}
\eta_n(\alpha)
:=
\sqrt{
\frac{
\log\!\left(2|\mathcal D||\mathcal B|/\alpha\right)
}{
2n
}
}.
\label{eq:simultaneous-radius}
\end{equation}
The radius \(\eta_n(\alpha)\) is the finite-sample uncertainty in the estimated
first-passage curves.  It decreases with the number of independent instances
and accounts for comparing every candidate design at every declared budget.

Define the lower and upper confidence bands
\begin{equation}
L_d(b)
:=
\max\{0,\widehat F_d(b)-\eta_n(\alpha)\},
\qquad
U_d(b)
:=
\min\{1,\widehat F_d(b)+\eta_n(\alpha)\}.
\label{eq:cdf-bands}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-sample selection certificate]
\label{thm:finite-sample}
With probability at least \(1-\alpha\),
\begin{equation}
L_d(b)\le F_d(b)\le U_d(b)
\label{eq:simultaneous-bands}
\end{equation}
simultaneously for every \(d\in\mathcal D\) and \(b\in\mathcal B\).

For a target reliability \(1-\delta\), define
\begin{equation}
T_d^-
:=
\inf\{b\in\mathcal B:U_d(b)\ge1-\delta\},
\qquad
T_d^+
:=
\inf\{b\in\mathcal B:L_d(b)\ge1-\delta\},
\label{eq:quantile-band}
\end{equation}
with \(\inf\varnothing=\infty\).  Then the grid-resolved proper time satisfies
\begin{equation}
T_d^-\le T_d(\delta)\le T_d^+,
\label{eq:proper-time-band}
\end{equation}
up to the resolution of \(\mathcal B\).

At a predeclared deployment horizon \(B\in\mathcal B\), design \(d\) is
statistically certified to meet the reliability target if
\begin{equation}
L_d(B)\ge1-\delta.
\label{eq:certified-admissibility}
\end{equation}

A certified design \(\widehat d\) is the unique proper-time winner over
\(\mathcal D\) if
\begin{equation}
T_{\widehat d}^{+}
<
\min_{d\neq\widehat d}T_d^{-}.
\label{eq:certified-winner}
\end{equation}
If this separation condition does not hold, the certificate returns
\emph{no winner}.
\end{theorem}

The certificate distinguishes three outcomes.  A design may fail to attain the
required reliability, it may attain the target without being statistically
separable from its competitors, or it may be certified as the unique
proper-time winner.  Increasing the number of retries improves the estimate
for each instance, but only additional independent task instances directly
shrink the distribution-free radius in
Equation~\eqref{eq:simultaneous-radius}.

\section{Tool Allocation and Four-Term Accounting}
\label{sec:tool-accounting}

\begin{definition}[Reference tool-call sequence]
\label{def:reference-tools}
Fix a finite tool set \(\mathcal A\).  Each task instance \(x\) is associated
with a reference tool-call sequence
\[
\mathbf a^\star(x)
=\bigl(a_1^\star(x),\ldots,a_{L(x)}^\star(x)\bigr),
\qquad a_k^\star(x)\in\mathcal A.
\]
It represents one successful ordered pattern of tool use for solving \(x\),
but need not be the unique successful sequence.  Let \(A_k^\star\) be the
reference tool at decision \(k\), and let \(H_k^\star\) contain the preceding
reference tool calls and outputs.  The pre-solution observation \(Z\) contains
the visible task request and any optional side information made available to
the tool-selection policy before the attempt begins; it contains neither a
candidate solution nor a verifier outcome.  Without side information, \(Z\) is
simply the visible task request.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Sequential tool-allocation policy]
\label{def:tool-policy}
Fix a backbone model \(M\) and a deployment design \(d\) built on \(M\).
Within one complete attempt, the next tool identity satisfies
\begin{equation}
A_k\sim q_d(\cdot\mid Z,H_k),
\qquad A_k\in\mathcal A,
\label{eq:tool-policy}
\end{equation}
where \(H_k\) contains the preceding tool calls and outputs.  Thus
\(q_d(a\mid z,h)\) is the allocation probability assigned to tool \(a\) at
that decision.  Write \(\mathbf A=(A_1,\ldots,A_{\widehat L})\) for the
complete executed sequence, where \(\widehat L\) is its possibly random number
of tool calls.  If tool outputs are deterministic and design \(d\) terminates
after \(L(x)\) calls along the reference histories, the probability that it
reproduces the reference sequence for \(x\) is
\begin{equation}
q_d^\star(x,z)
:=\prod_{k=1}^{L(x)}
q_d\!\left(a_k^\star(x)\mid z,h_k^\star(x)\right).
\label{eq:reference-sequence-probability}
\end{equation}
Interpretation of tool outputs and final-answer generation remain part of
design \(d\).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Variable-cost retry identity]
\label{prop:retry-first-passage}
Fix \((x,z)\).  For retry \(r\), let \(Y_r\) be the resulting artifact and
\(K_{d,r}>0\) its realized resource cost.  Assume that, conditional on
\((x,z)\), the pairs \(\bigl(K_{d,r},V(x,Y_r)\bigr)\) are IID across complete
retries.  Define
\[
\lambda_d(x,z):=\Prob(V(x,Y_r)=1\mid x,z)>0,
\qquad
\overline K_d(x,z):=\E[K_{d,r}\mid x,z],
\]
and let \(R_d(x,z):=\inf\{r\ge1:V(x,Y_r)=1\}\).  Then
\begin{equation}
B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(x)
=c_d(z)+\sum_{r=1}^{R_d(x,z)}K_{d,r},
\qquad
\E[B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(x)\mid x,z]
=c_d(z)+\frac{\overline K_d(x,z)}{\lambda_d(x,z)}.
\label{eq:variable-cost-first-passage}
\end{equation}
Here \(c_d(z)\ge0\) is only the incremental cost of acquiring the signal.  Set
\(c_d(z)=0\) when the signal is supplied with the task or its cost is already
included in \(K_{d,r}\).
\end{proposition}

The IID assumption means that every retry restarts the same frozen design with
fresh run randomness and carries no state across retries.  Stateful or adaptive
retry policies remain covered by direct proper-time measurement, but not by
Proposition~\ref{prop:retry-first-passage} without further analysis.

\subsection{Reference-sequence regime}

To obtain an exact accounting identity, suppose that reproducing
\(\mathbf a^\star(x)\) succeeds with probability \(p_d(x)>0\), executions that
deviate from it have zero verified-success probability, and the signal affects
success only through tool allocation.  Define \(t_0(d,x):=1/p_d(x)\).

\begin{proposition}[Tool-allocation first-passage identity]
\label{prop:tool-first-passage}
In the reference-sequence regime,
\begin{equation}
\lambda_d(x,z)=q_d^\star(x,z)p_d(x).
\label{eq:reference-success-probability}
\end{equation}
If the signal has no separate incremental cost, or that cost is included in
the retry ledger, then
\begin{equation}
\E[B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}\mid x,z]
=\overline K_d(x,z)\frac{t_0(d,x)}{q_d^\star(x,z)}.
\label{eq:tool-first-passage}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

We now introduce the reference distribution needed for the decomposition.  At
reference decision \(k\), let
\begin{equation}
\rho_k(a\mid z,h)
:=\Prob(A_k^\star=a\mid Z=z,H_k^\star=h,L\ge k).
\label{eq:reference-tool-posterior}
\end{equation}
This is a posterior over the next reference tool, not a distribution over tool
availability.  It may be one-hot when the reference tool is determined by the
observed context; the distributional notation also covers residual uncertainty
across instances with the same context.  Assume \(\rho_k(a\mid z,h)>0\) implies
\(q_d(a\mid z,h)>0\).  Define
\begin{align}
\mathcal H_{\mathrm{ref}}
&:=\sum_{k\ge1}\Prob(L\ge k)
H(A_k^\star\mid H_k^\star,L\ge k),\nonumber\\
G_Z
&:=\sum_{k\ge1}\Prob(L\ge k)
I(A_k^\star;Z\mid H_k^\star,L\ge k),\nonumber\\
\varepsilon_d
&:=\sum_{k\ge1}\Prob(L\ge k)
\E\!\left[
\KL\!\left(\rho_k(\cdot\mid Z,H_k^\star)
\Vert q_d(\cdot\mid Z,H_k^\star)\right)
\,\middle|\,L\ge k\right].
\label{eq:sequential-tool-mismatch}
\end{align}

\begin{lemma}[Sequential tool-allocation decomposition]
\label{lem:tool-cross-entropy}
The expected negative log probability assigned to the reference tool calls is
\begin{equation}
\E\!\left[-\sum_{k=1}^{L}
\log q_d(A_k^\star\mid Z,H_k^\star)\right]
=\mathcal H_{\mathrm{ref}}-G_Z+\varepsilon_d.
\label{eq:tool-cross-entropy}
\end{equation}
Thus \(G_Z\) measures how much the signal reduces uncertainty about appropriate
tool decisions, while \(\varepsilon_d\) measures the remaining mismatch with
the model policy.
\end{lemma}

Define the expected-log conditional first-passage diagnostic
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L(d):=\E_{X,Z}\!\left[
\log\E(B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}\mid X,Z)\right].
\label{eq:tool-risk}
\end{equation}
It is neither \(\E\log B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}\),
\(\log\E B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}\), nor \(\log T_d(\delta)\).

\begin{theorem}[Four-term tool-allocation decomposition]
\label{thm:four-term}
Let designs \(d_0,d\) share the task distribution, tool set, and reference
tool-call sequences, and use signals \(Z_0,Z\).  Suppose both satisfy the
reference-sequence regime and have no separate signal cost.  Define
\begin{align*}
C&:=\E_{X,Z_0}\log\overline K_{d_0}(X,Z_0)
-\E_{X,Z}\log\overline K_d(X,Z),\\
\Phi&:=\E_X\!\left[\log\frac{t_0(d_0,X)}{t_0(d,X)}\right].
\end{align*}
Then
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L(d_0)-\mathcal L(d)
=C+\Phi+G_Z-G_{Z_0}+\varepsilon_{d_0}-\varepsilon_d.
\label{eq:four-term-general}
\end{equation}
If \(Z_0\) is conditionally uninformative about every reference tool decision
and the baseline policy matches the corresponding prior, then
\(G_{Z_0}=\varepsilon_{d_0}=0\), and
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\Delta_{\mathrm{tool}}=C+\Phi+G_Z-\varepsilon_d.}
\label{eq:four-term-baseline}
\end{equation}
The four terms are relative mean retry cost, focused competence, signal
information about appropriate tool use, and sequential tool-policy mismatch.
Because the policy can change the retry-cost distribution, this is a
mechanistic accounting rather than a causal separation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Testable off-reference departure]
\label{prop:off-reference}
Let
\[
r_d^{\mathrm{alt}}(x,z)
:=\Prob(V(x,Y_r)=1,\mathbf A\ne\mathbf a^\star(x)\mid x,z)
\]
be the per-retry verified-success probability contributed by non-reference
executions.  Ignoring signal cost, define the log residual as the actual
conditional mean minus the reference-regime prediction:
\begin{equation}
r_d(x,z)=-\log\!\left(
1+\frac{r_d^{\mathrm{alt}}(x,z)}{q_d^\star(x,z)p_d(x)}
\right).
\label{eq:off-reference-residual}
\end{equation}
If the ratio inside the logarithm is at most \(\xi\), then
\(|r_d(x,z)|\le\log(1+\xi)\).  A separate signal cost breaks the exact
log-additivity.  The verifier still accepts every valid non-reference
execution; the proposition only tests the adequacy of the diagnostic regime.
\end{proposition}

Separating \(\overline K_d\) from \(t_0\) requires retry-level resource and
verification logs.  A policy change can alter both mean retry cost and
tool-policy mismatch, so the terms are not causal without randomized
interventions.  Proper time remains the model-selection criterion and is
measured directly; Theorem~\ref{thm:four-term} audits why expected log
conditional first-passage resource differs when its assumptions are adequate.

\begin{algorithm}[How to Pick the Model]
\label{alg:pick-model}
\begin{tabular}{@{}r p{0.86\linewidth}@{}}
\textit{Input:} &
Verifiable task family, candidate designs \(\mathcal D\), verifier \(V\),
resource unit, tolerance \(\delta\), horizon \(B\), budget grid \(\mathcal B\),
and confidence level \(1-\alpha\).\\[0.25em]
\textit{Output:} &
A statistically certified winner or ``no winner,'' plus an optional sequential
tool-allocation mechanism audit.\\[0.35em]
1 & \textit{Freeze the target.} Declare \(V\), the resource unit, \(\delta\),
\(B\), \(\mathcal B\), and the candidate menu.\\
2 & \textit{Measure first passage.} Stop each run at its first verified artifact
or at \(B\); retain failures as infinite and cluster paired runs by task.\\
3 & \textit{Construct bands.} Compute Equation~\eqref{eq:cluster-cdf}, the
simultaneous CDF bands, and \([T_d^-,T_d^+]\) for every design.\\
4 & \textit{Certify or abstain.} Name a winner only if it is admissible and its
upper proper-time bound lies below every competitor's lower bound.\\
5 & \textit{Audit the mechanism.} On disjoint calibration and confirmation data,
estimate reference tool-call probabilities, conditional success, variable
retry costs, and any incremental signal cost; test IID retries,
reference-sequence adequacy, and off-reference success.\\
6 & \textit{Explain, do not rerank.} Where the reference-sequence regime is
adequate, report the four terms, sequential tool-policy mismatch, and held-out
residual; keep the direct proper-time certificate as selection authority.
\end{tabular}
\end{algorithm}

The chain is explicit: Definition~\ref{def:proper-time} sets the deployment
estimand; Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-sample} selects or abstains;
Proposition~\ref{prop:retry-first-passage} links variable retry costs to mean
first passage; and Theorem~\ref{thm:four-term} supplies a scoped tool-policy
mechanism audit.
