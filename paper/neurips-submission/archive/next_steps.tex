\paragraph{Student-facing guide: what is changing.}
The old story was:
\[
    \textit{expected certified log-effort decomposes into cost, competence,
    routing information, and routing mismatch.}
\]
This is mathematically elegant, but the primary theorem currently treats the router as if it
directly allocates a mode-share vector \(q_z(s)\), and the effort improves or degrades
according to that share.
That is exactly right in a Kelly/portfolio-style allocation model, where computation is
literally split across mode-specialized strategies.
It is less faithful to the main AutoResearch protocol, where the router observes a cheap
signal \(Z\), then chooses a model, retry depth, fallback rule, or harness action.

The new story should be:
\begin{center}
    \textit{for verifier-backed agent systems, practitioners should compare
    model/harness designs by deployment loss and selection regret, not scalar pass rate.
    Latent modes, cheap signals, mutual information, and posterior calibration are useful
    because they make the deployment decision cheaper to estimate and easier to diagnose.}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Main principle.}
The central object is not:
\[
    I(S;Z)
    \quad\text{or}\quad
    \mathbb E_Z \KL(\pi_Z\|q_Z).
\]
Those are diagnostics.
The deployment object is:
\[
    J_\ell(r;Z)
    =
    \mathbb E_Z
    \sum_{a\in\mathcal A} r(a\mid Z)
    \sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\Pr(S=s\mid Z)\ell(a,s),
\]
where:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
    \mathcal A & \text{is the deployable action menu},\\
    a          & \text{is a model, retry policy, fallback rule, subagent, or harness},\\
    S          & \text{is a latent task mode},\\
    Z          & \text{is a cheap information state available before a costly decision},\\
    \ell(a,s)  & \text{is the deployment loss of action \(a\) on mode \(s\)}.
\end{array}
\]

\paragraph{The new slogan.}
The paper should say:
\[
    \boxed{
    \textit{We turn model/harness choice from benchmark racing into
    decision-theoretic performance accounting.}
    }
\]
The accounting estimates:
\[
    \ell(a,s),\qquad
    \Pr(S=s\mid Z),\qquad
    \mathrm{VOI}_{\ell,\mathcal A}(Z),\qquad
    \mathrm{Reg}_{\ell}(r),\qquad
    \mathcal R_{\mathrm{sel}}(N).
\]
In words:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
    \ell(a,s) & \text{which actions work on which modes and at what cost},\\
    \Pr(S=s\mid Z) & \text{what the cheap signal reveals},\\
    \mathrm{VOI}_{\ell,\mathcal A}(Z) & \text{whether the signal changes the best decision},\\
    \mathrm{Reg}_{\ell}(r) & \text{how much the learned router leaves on the table},\\
    \mathcal R_{\mathrm{sel}}(N) & \text{how cheaply the evaluation protocol finds a good design}.
\end{array}
\]

\paragraph{The five empirical messages.}
The experiments should establish five messages separately.
Do not organize the empirical section as only:
\[
    \text{``our router improves over the best single model.''}
\]
That is too narrow.
Instead, the paper should establish:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
    \textbf{Message 1} &
    \text{practitioners should optimize certified effort, not raw pass rate},\\
    \textbf{Message 2} &
    \text{model/harness choice is mode-dependent, so scalar leaderboards hide structure},\\
    \textbf{Message 3} &
    \text{cheap pre-run signals can create routing opportunity},\\
    \textbf{Message 4} &
    \text{posterior/mode information is useful, but decision value and router regret
    are deployment-relevant},\\
    \textbf{Message 5} &
    \text{decomposition-guided evaluation can be cheaper than black-box racing}.
\end{array}
\]
Each message needs its own statistic, negative control, and failure interpretation.

\paragraph{How to rewrite the paper's scope.}
The current paper should become a measurement-and-methods paper, not a grand theory of
agents.
The old broader paper contains useful background vocabulary, but the main NeurIPS paper
should not try to simultaneously solve:
\[
    \text{task equivalence},\quad
    \text{transfer},\quad
    \text{multi-agent thresholds},\quad
    \text{universal search},\quad
    \text{agent ontology},\quad
    \text{model routing}.
\]
The main paper should focus on:
\[
    \boxed{
    \text{cheap model/harness selection for verifier-backed agent systems.}
    }
\]
The older broad theory can appear only as:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
    \text{one short related-work paragraph},\\
    \text{one optional appendix on Kelly-style allocation},\\
    \text{one future-work paragraph on task equivalence and transfer}.
\end{array}
\]

\paragraph{New paper thesis.}
A good thesis sentence is:
\begin{quote}
\itshape
Given a verifier-backed task family and a finite menu of models, retry depths,
fallback rules, and routers, we estimate deployment loss, routing opportunity,
learned-router regret, and selection regret from reusable mode-conditioned pilots.
\end{quote}
This is concrete, testable, and close to practice.

\paragraph{New abstract skeleton.}
A better abstract should say:
\begin{quote}
Agent builders often compare models by end-to-end benchmark racing, but a scalar pass
rate hides cost, persistence, task heterogeneity, and routing failure.
For verifier-backed agent systems, the deployment question is which model or harness action
minimizes verified effort on the current instance.
We formulate this as finite-action selection under latent task modes.
Given a mode-conditioned loss matrix and a cheap information state \(Z\), the gain of any
routed policy decomposes exactly into routing opportunity, the value of the signal for action
choice, minus learned-router regret.
Information-theoretic quantities such as \(I(S;Z)\) and
\(\mathbb E\KL(\pi_Z\|q_Z)\) become diagnostics: they measure mode identifiability and
posterior calibration, but decision value and regret are the deployment-facing quantities.
We instantiate the framework on AutoResearch-style verifier-backed code-editing tasks,
where no-stop trajectories reveal that first-passage success can saturate while occupancy
continues to separate modes.
The resulting accounting tells a builder whether to deploy the best single model, build a
router, improve the diagnostic probe, or change the model/harness menu.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{New contribution list.}
Replace the current contribution list with:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.25em,itemsep=0.15em]
    \item \textbf{Deployment-loss formulation.}
    We define finite-action model/harness selection under verifier-backed deployment loss,
    including first-passage, occupancy, additive cost, and tail-risk variants.
    \item \textbf{Routing opportunity and regret identity.}
    We prove that the improvement of any learned router equals the value of the cheap signal
    for action choice minus the learned router's regret relative to the oracle signal router.
    \item \textbf{Information diagnostics.}
    We show how mutual information and posterior KL diagnose mode identifiability and
    calibration, and how posterior KL can bound decision regret, but we distinguish these
    from deployment value.
    \item \textbf{Decomposition-guided evaluation.}
    We give a structured pilot protocol that estimates reusable model-mode losses and signal
    posteriors, then scores many candidate designs without black-box racing every design.
    \item \textbf{AutoResearch study.}
    We test the accounting on AutoResearch-like verifier-backed code-editing tasks, including
    pass-rate versus certified-effort rank reversals, mode-dependent competence, cheap-signal
    value, dynamic post-failure routing, and selection-regret curves.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What to demote.}
Demote the old four-term theorem:
\[
    \Delta
    =
    \log\frac{\kappa(M_0)}{\kappa(M)}
    +
    \sum_s\pi_s\log\frac{p_M(s)}{p_{M_0}(s)}
    +
    I(S;Z)
    -
    \mathbb E_Z\KL(\pi_Z\|q_Z)
\]
from the main theorem to a special case.
It is correct and useful when:
\[
    q_z(s)=\text{fraction of compute allocated to mode-specialized strategy \(s\)}.
\]
Examples where this interpretation is exact or nearly exact include:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
    \text{proof search} & \text{allocate branches across induction, rewriting, library search},\\
    \text{program repair} & \text{allocate attempts across dependency, type, invariant fixes},\\
    \text{AutoResearch branching} & \text{allocate candidate edits across LR, optimizer, capacity,
    schedule families}.
\end{array}
\]
But in the current main AutoResearch protocol, the router chooses an action.
Therefore the primary theorem should be action-level.

\paragraph{New mathematical objects.}
Let:
\[
    X\sim\mu,\qquad
    S=\sigma(X)\in\mathcal S,\qquad
    Z=Z(X)\in\mathcal Z.
\]
Here \(S\) is a latent bottleneck mode and \(Z\) is a cheap information state.
The deployable action menu is:
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal A=\{&\text{single model},\ \text{model + retry depth},\
    \ \text{cheap-then-expensive fallback},\\
    &\text{router over models},\ \text{subagent harness},\
    \ \text{verifier horizon}\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For the current AutoResearch paper, start with:
\[
    \mathcal A_{\mathrm{simple}}
    =
    \{
    \text{GPT-5.4 mini},
    \text{GPT-5.3 Codex},
    \text{GPT-5.3 Codex Spark},
    \text{Claude Sonnet}
    \}.
\]
Then add sequential actions later:
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal A_{\mathrm{seq}}=\{&\text{single model},\
    \ \text{cheap model for \(k\) steps then stop},\\
    &\text{cheap model for \(k\) steps then fixed fallback},\\
    &\text{cheap model for \(k\) steps then posterior fallback}\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

\paragraph{Define trajectory quantities.}
For action \(a\), instance \(x\), and horizon \(H\), let:
\[
    \tau_a(x)
    =
    \min\{h\le H:V_{\mathrm{succ}}(x,y_{a,h})=1\},
\]
with \(\tau_a(x)=\infty\) if no verified artifact appears by \(H\).
Let:
\[
    C_{a,\tau\wedge H}(x)
    =
    \text{cumulative wall-clock/API/token/verifier cost to \(\tau\wedge H\)}.
\]
Let:
\[
    F_a(s,H)
    =
    \Pr(\tau_a\le H\mid S=s)
\]
be first-passage success.
Let:
\[
    O_a^\delta(s,H)
    =
    \mathbb E\left[
        \frac{1}{H}\sum_{h=1}^H
        \mathbf 1\{R_{a,h}(X)\ge \delta\}
        \,\middle|\,
        S=s
    \right]
\]
be threshold occupancy, where \(R_{a,h}(X)\) is relative validation-loss improvement at step \(h\).
Let:
\[
    C_a(s,H)
    =
    \mathbb E[C_{a,\tau\wedge H}(X)\mid S=s].
\]

\paragraph{Define multiple losses, not one.}
The main theorem can use one loss \(\ell\), while the experiments report several.
Use:
\[
    \ell_{\mathrm{hit}}(a,s)
    =
    \log C_a(s,H)-\log(F_a(s,H)\vee \alpha).
\]
This is close to the current first-passage theorem.
But AutoResearch also needs:
\[
    \ell_{\mathrm{occ}}(a,s)
    =
    \log C_a^{\mathrm{full}}(s,H)-\log(O_a^\delta(s,H)\vee \alpha),
\]
where \(C_a^{\mathrm{full}}\) is the cost of the full no-stop trajectory.
Also report:
\[
    \ell_{\mathrm{add}}(a,s)
    =
    \mathbb E[C_{a,\tau\wedge H}\mid a,s]
    +
    \lambda\,\Pr(\tau_a>H\mid a,s),
\]
and, if sample size permits:
\[
    \ell_{\mathrm{tail}}(a)
    =
    \mathrm{CVaR}_{0.95}(C_{a,\tau\wedge H})
    +
    \lambda\,\Pr(\tau_a>H).
\]
The first two are the main paper losses.
The additive and tail losses are robustness checks.

\paragraph{Why keep both first-hit and occupancy.}
First-hit success answers:
\[
    \textit{does the agent ever cross the verification threshold?}
\]
Occupancy answers:
\[
    \textit{does the agent produce stable verified progress over the trajectory?}
\]
In AutoResearch, these can differ.
A model can cross the threshold once because of a lucky local edit and then fail to sustain
improvement.
A different model can cross later but maintain durable progress.
Therefore the empirical section should not only report:
\[
    F_a(s,H).
\]
It should report:
\[
    F_a(s,H),
    \qquad
    C_a(s,H),
    \qquad
    O_a^\delta(s,H),
    \qquad
    \ell_{\mathrm{hit}}(a,s),
    \qquad
    \ell_{\mathrm{occ}}(a,s).
\]

\paragraph{Define the action-level objective.}
For a deterministic no-routing action \(a\), define:
\[
    J_\ell(a)
    =
    \sum_s \pi_s \ell(a,s).
\]
For a possibly randomized router \(r(a\mid z)\), define:
\[
    J_\ell(r;Z)
    =
    \mathbb E_Z
    \sum_a r(a\mid Z)
    \sum_s \pi_z(s)\ell(a,s),
    \qquad
    \pi_z(s)=\Pr(S=s\mid Z=z).
\]
This is the expected deployment loss of the routed system.

\paragraph{Best no-routing action.}
Define:
\[
    a^\star_{\emptyset}
    \in
    \argmin_{a\in\mathcal A}
    \sum_s \pi_s\ell(a,s).
\]
This is the best single global action.
It is the honest no-router baseline.

\paragraph{Oracle mode action.}
Define:
\[
    a^\star_S(s)
    \in
    \argmin_{a\in\mathcal A}\ell(a,s).
\]
This is the action that would be chosen if the true mode were revealed.
It gives the maximum mode-conditioned opportunity:
\[
    \mathrm{CA}_{\ell}
    =
    J_\ell(a^\star_{\emptyset})
    -
    \sum_s \pi_s\min_a \ell(a,s).
\]
Call this \emph{comparative advantage}.
If:
\[
    \mathrm{CA}_{\ell}\approx 0,
\]
then routing is not worth building, no matter how well \(Z\) predicts the mode.
One action is already globally optimal.

\paragraph{Oracle signal action.}
Define:
\[
    a^\star_Z(z)
    \in
    \argmin_{a\in\mathcal A}
    \sum_s \pi_z(s)\ell(a,s).
\]
This is the best action one could choose after observing the cheap signal \(Z=z\).
Its value is:
\[
    \mathrm{VOI}_{\ell,\mathcal A}(Z)
    =
    J_\ell(a^\star_{\emptyset})
    -
    J_\ell(a^\star_Z;Z).
\]
Equivalently:
\[
    \mathrm{VOI}_{\ell,\mathcal A}(Z)
    =
    \min_a \mathbb E[\ell(a,S)]
    -
    \mathbb E_Z
    \min_a \mathbb E[\ell(a,S)\mid Z].
\]
This is the most important replacement for raw mutual information.
It answers:
\[
    \textit{how much does this cheap signal improve the model/harness decision?}
\]

\paragraph{Learned-router regret.}
For a learned router \(r\), define:
\[
    \mathrm{Reg}_{\ell}(r;Z)
    =
    J_\ell(r;Z)-J_\ell(a^\star_Z;Z).
\]
This is the amount of deployment loss left on the table because the learned router is not
the oracle signal router.

\paragraph{Primary theorem.}
The primary theorem should be:
\[
\boxed{
    J_\ell(a^\star_{\emptyset}) - J_\ell(r;Z)
    =
    \underbrace{
    J_\ell(a^\star_{\emptyset})-J_\ell(a^\star_Z;Z)
    }_{\text{routing opportunity / value of signal}}
    -
    \underbrace{
    \bigl[
    J_\ell(r;Z)-J_\ell(a^\star_Z;Z)
    \bigr]
    }_{\text{learned-router regret}}.
}
\]
This identity is simple but extremely useful.
It separates:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
    \text{opportunity} & \text{whether routing is worth engineering},\\
    \text{regret} & \text{whether the learned router captures the available value}.
\end{array}
\]

\paragraph{Interpretation of the theorem.}
There are four possible outcomes:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
    \mathrm{VOI}\approx 0 &
    \text{do not route; use the best single action},\\
    \mathrm{VOI}>0,\ \mathrm{Reg}\approx 0 &
    \text{deploy the learned router},\\
    \mathrm{VOI}>0,\ \mathrm{Reg}\text{ large} &
    \text{the signal is valuable but the router is bad; fix calibration/decision rule},\\
    \mathrm{CA}>0,\ \mathrm{VOI}\approx 0 &
    \text{different modes want different actions, but \(Z\) cannot identify them;
    design a better probe}.
\end{array}
\]

\paragraph{Nonnegativity of value of information.}
Prove:
\[
    \mathrm{VOI}_{\ell,\mathcal A}(Z)\ge 0.
\]
Proof:
\[
    \mathbb E_Z \min_a \mathbb E[\ell(a,S)\mid Z]
    \le
    \mathbb E_Z \mathbb E[\ell(a^\star_{\emptyset},S)\mid Z]
    =
    \mathbb E[\ell(a^\star_{\emptyset},S)].
\]
Therefore observing \(Z\) cannot hurt an oracle decision-maker who is free to ignore it.

\paragraph{Zero value condition.}
If the same action is optimal for almost every signal:
\[
    a^\star
    \in
    \argmin_a \mathbb E[\ell(a,S)\mid Z=z]
    \qquad
    \text{for almost every }z,
\]
then:
\[
    \mathrm{VOI}_{\ell,\mathcal A}(Z)=0.
\]
This is the clean mathematical reason that high mode information is not automatically useful.

\paragraph{Where mutual information belongs.}
Mutual information is:
\[
    I(S;Z)
    =
    H(S)-\mathbb E_ZH(S\mid Z).
\]
It answers:
\[
    \textit{does \(Z\) reveal the latent bottleneck?}
\]
It does not answer:
\[
    \textit{does \(Z\) improve the model/harness choice?}
\]
Therefore report:
\[
    I(S;Z)
    \quad\text{next to}\quad
    \mathrm{VOI}_{\ell,\mathcal A}(Z).
\]
A strong conceptual plot is:
\[
    x=I(S;Z),
    \qquad
    y=\mathrm{VOI}_{\ell,\mathcal A}(Z),
\]
across signal ablations, signal corruptions, mode merges, and deployment-prior reweightings.

\paragraph{Where KL calibration belongs.}
Let a posterior router output \(q_z(s)\), and let:
\[
    a_q(z)
    \in
    \argmin_a \sum_s q_z(s)\ell(a,s).
\]
The induced decision regret is:
\[
    \mathrm{Reg}_{\ell}(q)
    =
    \mathbb E_Z
    \left[
    \sum_s \pi_z(s)\ell(a_q(z),s)
    -
    \min_a \sum_s \pi_z(s)\ell(a,s)
    \right].
\]
If:
\[
    0\le \ell(a,s)\le L
\]
for all \(a,s\), then:
\[
    \mathrm{Reg}_{\ell}(q)
    \le
    L\sqrt{2\,\mathbb E_Z\KL(\pi_Z\|q_Z)}.
\]
Proof sketch:
\[
\begin{aligned}
    &\sum_s \pi_z(s)\ell(a_q(z),s)
    -
    \min_a \sum_s\pi_z(s)\ell(a,s)\\
    &\qquad\le
    2L\,\mathrm{TV}(\pi_z,q_z)
    \le
    L\sqrt{2\KL(\pi_z\|q_z)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Then average over \(Z\) and use Jensen.
This gives a principled role to KL:
\[
    \textit{posterior calibration can control regret, but regret is the deployment metric.}
\]

\paragraph{When the old KL theorem is exact.}
Keep the old theorem as:
\[
    \textbf{Corollary: Kelly-style mode-share allocation.}
\]
In that special case, the action is not a single model.
The action is a compute-share vector:
\[
    q_z\in\Delta(\mathcal S),
\]
and the time under mode \(s\) satisfies:
\[
    T_q(s,z)\propto \frac{1}{q_z(s)}.
\]
Then:
\[
    \mathbb E[-\log q_Z(S)]
    =
    \mathbb E_ZH(\pi_Z)+\mathbb E_Z\KL(\pi_Z\|q_Z),
\]
and one nat of KL mismatch literally costs one nat of log-effort.
This is valuable as a limiting allocation model, but it should not be the main theorem for
a start-of-run model router.

\paragraph{Step-by-step paper rewrite.}
The student should rewrite the paper in this order.

\paragraph{Step 1: Rewrite the introduction.}
The opening should not be:
\begin{quote}
``we decompose model-aware log-effort into four terms.''
\end{quote}
The opening should be:
\begin{quote}
``benchmark racing is an expensive and low-resolution way to choose
models and harnesses for verifier-backed agents.''
\end{quote}
Then argue:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
    \text{raw pass rate hides cost} &
    \text{two models may both pass, but one is much cheaper or faster},\\
    \text{raw pass rate hides persistence} &
    \text{one model may hit threshold once, another may stay above threshold},\\
    \text{leaderboards hide mode structure} &
    \text{the global winner may fail on an important submode},\\
    \text{mode information is not enough} &
    \text{the signal must change the best deployment action},\\
    \text{racing is expensive} &
    \text{a large design menu is combinatorial, but model-mode cells are reusable}.
\end{array}
\]

\paragraph{Step 2: Rewrite the formal setup.}
Define:
\[
    \text{verifiable task},\quad
    \text{instance},\quad
    \text{mode},\quad
    \text{action menu},\quad
    \text{deployment loss}.
\]
Do not define the router first.
Define the deployment decision first:
\[
    Z \longrightarrow a.
\]
Then define posterior \(q(s\mid Z)\) only as one way to implement that decision.

\paragraph{Step 3: Replace the primary theorem.}
Move the current four-term theorem after the action-level theorem.
The main theorem should be:
\[
    \text{routing gain}=\text{routing opportunity}-\text{router regret}.
\]
Then add:
\[
    \mathrm{VOI}_{\ell,\mathcal A}(Z),
    \qquad
    \mathrm{CA}_\ell,
    \qquad
    \mathrm{Reg}_\ell(r).
\]
The current \(I(S;Z)\) and KL terms should appear in a subsection called:
\[
    \textbf{Information diagnostics and calibration.}
\]

\paragraph{Step 4: Rewrite the algorithm.}
The old algorithm estimates:
\[
    p_M(s),\quad \kappa(M),\quad I(S;Z),\quad \KL(\pi_Z\|q_Z).
\]
The new algorithm should estimate:
\[
    \ell(a,s),\quad
    \pi(s\mid z),\quad
    \mathrm{CA}_\ell,\quad
    \mathrm{VOI}_{\ell,\mathcal A}(Z),\quad
    \mathrm{Reg}_\ell(r),\quad
    \mathcal R_{\mathrm{sel}}(N).
\]
A clean version is:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
    \textbf{Input} &
    \text{model/harness action menu \(\mathcal A\), modes \(\mathcal S\),
    signal sets \(\mathcal Z\), verifier, budget},\\
    \textbf{1. Structured pilot} &
    \text{run model-mode no-stop trajectories to estimate losses},\\
    \textbf{2. Probe pilot} &
    \text{collect cheap \(Z\) and fit/calibrate \(q(s\mid Z)\)},\\
    \textbf{3. Accounting} &
    \text{compute \(\mathrm{CA}\), \(\mathrm{VOI}\), router regret},\\
    \textbf{4. Design scoring} &
    \text{score many candidate designs analytically},\\
    \textbf{5. Confirmation} &
    \text{evaluate top finalists end-to-end on fresh holdout},\\
    \textbf{6. Residual audit} &
    \text{compare predicted and observed deployment loss}.
\end{array}
\]

\paragraph{Step 5: Rewrite the experiment section around claims.}
The empirical section should have five blocks:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
    \textbf{Experiment A} & \text{pass rate versus certified effort},\\

\paragraph{No-stop trajectory logging.}
For each instance \(x_i\), model/action \(a\), and horizon \(H=24\):
\[
    \text{run the full trajectory even after first success}.
\]
At each step \(h\), log:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
    y_{i,h} & \text{proposed edited \texttt{train.py}},\\
    L_{i,h} & \text{validation loss},\\
    R_{i,h}=(L_{\mathrm{base}}-L_{i,h})/L_{\mathrm{base}} & \text{relative improvement},\\
    V_{i,h}=\mathbf 1\{R_{i,h}\ge \delta\} & \text{thresholded success},\\
    c_{i,h}^{\mathrm{wall}} & \text{incremental wall-clock cost},\\
    c_{i,h}^{\mathrm{api}} & \text{API cost if available},\\
    c_{i,h}^{\mathrm{tok}} & \text{token cost},\\
    e_{i,h} & \text{edit validity / parse / apply status}.
\end{array}
\]
If an edit is invalid, count the cost and record an unsuccessful no-op step.
Do not abort the whole trajectory because of one invalid structured edit.

\paragraph{Reconstruct outcomes offline.}
For each trajectory:
\[
    \tau_i
    =
    \min\{h:V_{i,h}=1\}.
\]
Then:
\[
    F_a(s,H)
    =
    \frac{1}{n_{a,s}}\sum_{i:a_i=a,S_i=s}
    \mathbf 1\{\tau_i\le H\},
\]
\[
    C_a(s,H)
    =
    \frac{1}{n_{a,s}}\sum_{i:a_i=a,S_i=s}
    C_{i,\tau_i\wedge H},
\]
\[
    O_a^\delta(s,H)
    =
    \frac{1}{n_{a,s}}\sum_{i:a_i=a,S_i=s}
    \frac{1}{H}\sum_{h=1}^H V_{i,h}.
\]
Also estimate the survival curve:
\[
    F_a(s,h)
    =
    \Pr(\tau_a\le h\mid s),
    \qquad
    h=1,\ldots,H,
\]
and the empirical hazard:
\[
    \lambda_a(s,h)
    =
    \Pr(\tau_a=h\mid \tau_a\ge h,a,s).
\]

Goal:

    \quad

    \quad

    \quad

Optimize cross-entropy:
\[

\paragraph{Prior correction.}
If the pilot is mode-balanced but deployment is not, correct the posterior.
If \(\tilde\pi\) is the pilot prior and \(\pi\) is the deployment prior:
\[
    \hat q_\pi(s\mid z)
    =
    \frac{(\pi_s/\tilde\pi_s)\hat q_{\tilde\pi}(s\mid z)}
    {\sum_t(\pi_t/\tilde\pi_t)\hat q_{\tilde\pi}(t\mid z)}.
\]
This matters.
Otherwise the router is calibrated to the artificial pilot mixture.

\paragraph{Convert posterior to action.}
For each signal \(z\), choose:
\[
    \hat a(z)
    =
    \argmin_a\sum_s\hat q(s\mid z)\hat\ell(a,s).
\]
This is the deployable router.
The posterior \(q(s\mid z)\) is not the final product.
The final product is:
\[
    z\mapsto \hat a(z).
\]

\paragraph{Estimate value of information.}
For each signal set \(Z\), compute:
\[
    \widehat{\mathrm{VOI}}_{\ell,\mathcal A}(Z)
    =
    \min_a\sum_s\hat\pi_s\hat\ell(a,s)
    -
    \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
    \min_a\sum_s\hat q(s\mid Z_i)\hat\ell(a,s).
\]
Then subtract signal cost:
\[
    \widehat{\mathrm{NVOI}}(Z)
    =
    \widehat{\mathrm{VOI}}(Z)-\hat c(Z).
\]
For the baseline-only probe, \(c(Z)\) is usually small but not zero.

\paragraph{Practical note on oracle signal.}
The true \(\pi(s\mid z)\) is unknown for continuous \(Z\).
So the oracle signal router is estimated.
Use a cross-fitted high-quality reference posterior:
\[
    \hat \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(s\mid z)
\]
trained on \((Z,S)\) but evaluated on held-out folds.
Then:
\[
    \hat a_Z^\star(z)
    =
    \argmin_a\sum_s\hat\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(s\mid z)\hat\ell(a,s).
\]
Call this an \emph{estimated oracle signal router}, not an exact oracle.
Report bootstrap intervals.

\paragraph{High-information low-value construction.}
Find or create two modes \(s_1,s_2\) such that:
\[
    \argmin_a\ell(a,s_1)=\argmin_a\ell(a,s_2).
\]
Use a signal that distinguishes \(s_1\) from \(s_2\).
Then:
\[
    I(S;Z)\text{ high},
    \qquad
    \mathrm{VOI}_\ell(Z)\approx 0.
\]
This demonstrates:
\[
    \textit{mode identification is not enough if the action does not change.}
\]

\paragraph{Low-information high-value construction.}
Let \(H\) be a rare hard mode:
\[
    \Pr(H)=\rho,\qquad \rho\ll 1.
\]
Suppose the cheap action fails badly on \(H\), but the expensive action succeeds.
A weak detector \(Z\) with moderate true-positive rate and low false-positive rate can have:
\[
    I(S;Z)\text{ modest}
\]
but:
\[
    \mathrm{VOI}_\ell(Z)\text{ large}.
\]
Implement this in AutoResearch by reweighting:
\[
    \pi_\rho=(1-\rho)\pi_{\mathrm{easy}}+\rho\pi_{\mathrm{hard}},
\]
where hard is capacity-sensitive or schedule-sensitive.
Then plot:
\[
    \rho\mapsto I(S;Z),
    \qquad
    \rho\mapsto \mathrm{VOI}_\ell(Z).
\]

\paragraph{Experiment F: KL calibration versus decision regret.}
Goal:
\[
    \text{show that posterior calibration is useful, but deployment regret is primary.}
\]
Given a reference posterior \(\hat q(s\mid z)\), create perturbations:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
    \text{temperature scaling} &
    q_T(s\mid z)\propto q(s\mid z)^{1/T},\\
    \text{uniform mixing} &
    q_\eta=(1-\eta)q+\eta\mathrm{Unif},\\
    \text{top-one preserving noise} &
    \text{change probabilities but keep the same induced action},\\
    \text{mode swaps} &
    \text{swap probability mass between selected mode pairs},\\
    \text{boundary flips} &
    \text{small perturbations near action decision boundaries}.
\end{array}
\]
For each perturbed posterior, compute:
\[
    x=\mathbb E_Z\KL(\pi_Z\|q_Z),
    \qquad
    y=\mathrm{Reg}_{\ell}(q).
\]
Plot \(y\) against \(x\).

\paragraph{Expected KL/regret result.}
The plot should show:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
    \text{KL often correlates with regret} & \text{calibration is useful},\\
    \text{high KL, low regret} & \text{if the induced action is unchanged},\\
    \text{low KL, high regret} & \text{if a small error flips a high-stakes action}.
\end{array}
\]
This justifies the paper's revised wording:
\[
    \textit{KL is a calibration diagnostic; decision regret is the deployment quantity.}
\]

\paragraph{Experiment G: pre-run routing versus post-failure routing.}
This experiment answers:
\[
    \textit{does the \(Z\mapsto\Pr(S\mid Z)\) story only apply to hard tasks
    or retries after failure?}
\]
The answer should be tested, not assumed.

Define:
\[
    Z_0=\text{baseline-only pre-run probe}.
\]
Choose a cheap default action \(a_0\), usually the cheapest plausible model.
Run \(a_0\) for:
\[
    k\in\{1,2,4,8\}
\]
steps.
For unsolved instances, record:
\[
    Z_k
    =
    \left(
    Z_0,
    \tau_{a_0}>k,
    L_1,\ldots,L_k,
    \Delta L_1,\ldots,\Delta L_k,
    \text{edit validity},
    \text{failure type},
    \text{cost so far}
    \right).
\]
Fit:
\[
    \hat q_k(s\mid Z_k)
    \approx
    \Pr(S=s\mid Z_0,H_k,\tau>k).
\]

\paragraph{Why failure is informative.}
If the cheap model has success curve \(F_{a_0}(s,k)\), then:
\[
    \Pr(S=s\mid \tau_{a_0}>k)
    \propto
    \pi_s\bigl(1-F_{a_0}(s,k)\bigr).
\]
So if the cheap model solves easy modes quickly but fails on hard modes, failure moves
posterior mass toward hard modes.
This is the cleanest mathematical reason that post-failure routing can matter more than
pre-run routing.

\paragraph{Estimate remaining loss after \(k\) steps.}
Do not reuse the original \(\ell(a,s)\) blindly.
After \(k\) failed cheap steps, the population is conditional:
\[
    S\sim\Pr(S\mid Z_0,H_k,\tau>k).
\]
Estimate:
\[
    \ell^{\mathrm{rem}}_k(a,s)
    =
    \text{expected remaining deployment loss of fallback action \(a\)
    after \(k\) cheap steps in mode \(s\)}.
\]
Practical implementation:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
    \text{after \(k\) cheap steps} & \text{try Codex},\\
    \text{after \(k\) cheap steps} & \text{try Spark},\\
    \text{after \(k\) cheap steps} & \text{try Sonnet}.
\end{array}
\]
This requires either randomized fallback evaluation or matched replay.
Avoid evaluating fallback only on the actions the old policy happened to choose, because
that creates confounding.

\paragraph{Compare sequential policies.}
Report:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
    \textbf{Best single} &
    \text{one model from start to horizon},\\
    \textbf{Pre-run router} &
    \hat a(Z_0)\text{ from start},\\
    \textbf{Cheap-then-fixed fallback} &
    a_0\text{ for \(k\) steps, then fixed expensive action if unsolved},\\
    \textbf{Cheap-then-posterior fallback} &
    a_0\text{ for \(k\) steps, then }\hat a_k(Z_k)\text{ if unsolved}.
\end{array}
\]
The comparison should include the cost of obtaining \(Z_k\):
\[
    \text{failure information is not free.}
\]

\paragraph{Sequential objective.}
For this dynamic experiment, use an additive objective first:
\[
    J_{\mathrm{seq}}(\pi)
    =
    \mathbb E
    \left[
        C_{\mathrm{total}}(\pi)
        +
        \lambda\mathbf 1\{\tau_\pi>H\}
    \right].
\]
The log-effort objective can be reported second.
The additive objective is simpler for fallback policies because costs add naturally over stages.

\paragraph{Experiment G pass condition.}
Promote the post-failure claim if:
\[
    J(\text{cheap then posterior fallback})
    <
    J(\text{cheap then fixed fallback})
\]
and:
\[
    \mathrm{VOI}(Z_k\mid \tau>k)>0
\]
after accounting for the cost of the first \(k\) steps.

\paragraph{Experiment G interpretations.}
If \(Z_0\) has value and \(Z_k\) adds little:
\[
    \text{pre-run probe is enough.}
\]
If \(Z_0\) has little value but \(Z_k\) has large value:
\[
    \text{the posterior story is mainly a retry-after-failure story.}
\]
If neither has value:
\[
    \text{either the modes are not decision-relevant, the signal is weak, or the action menu lacks
    comparative advantage.}
\]

\paragraph{Experiment H: difficulty sweep.}
Goal:
\[
    \text{test whether posterior routing only matters for difficult tasks.}
\]
Vary:
\[
    \delta,
    \qquad
    H,
    \qquad
    \text{inner verifier budget},
    \qquad
    \text{defect severity},
    \qquad
    \text{hard-mode prior }\rho.
\]
For each setting compute:
\[
    \mathrm{CA}_{\ell},
    \qquad
    \mathrm{VOI}(Z_0),
    \qquad
    \mathrm{VOI}(Z_k\mid \tau>k),
    \qquad
    \mathrm{Reg}_{\ell}(\hat r).
\]
Expected regimes:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
    \text{too easy} &
    \mathrm{CA}\approx 0,\ \mathrm{VOI}\approx 0,\\
    \text{intermediate heterogeneous} &
    \mathrm{CA}>0,\ \mathrm{VOI}>0,\\
    \text{too hard} &
    \text{all actions fail or one expensive action dominates},\\
    \text{rare hard mode} &
    \text{VOI can be large even if \(I(S;Z)\) is modest}.
\end{array}
\]
This is one of the most important conceptual experiments.

\paragraph{Experiment I: decomposition-guided evaluation versus black-box racing.}
Goal:
\[
    \text{show that the accounting is not just explanatory, but cheaper for model/harness selection.}
\]
Construct a design menu:
\[
\mathcal D
=
\{
\text{model }M,
\text{retry depth }D,
\text{fallback rule }f,
\text{signal set }Z,
\text{router temperature }T,
\text{horizon }H,
\text{loss convention }\ell
\}.
\]
Even a modest menu becomes large:
\[
    |\mathcal D|
    =
    |\mathcal M|\cdot|D|\cdot|f|\cdot|Z|\cdot|T|\cdot|H|.
\]

\paragraph{Reference evaluation.}
First create a high-quality reference estimate:
\[
    J_{\mathrm{ref}}(d)
\]
for each design \(d\) using a large held-out set or the best available pooled evaluation.
This is not used by the selection methods; it is used to score their selected design.

\paragraph{Black-box racing baseline.}
At budget \(N\):
\[
    \text{sample complete designs end-to-end},
\]
estimate:
\[
    \hat J_N(d),
\]
choose:
\[
    \hat d_N^{\mathrm{race}}
    =
    \argmin_d\hat J_N(d).
\]
Include:
\[
    \text{uniform racing},
    \qquad
    \text{successive halving},
    \qquad
    \text{random search},
    \qquad
    \text{Hyperband-style racing if easy to implement}.
\]

\paragraph{Decomposition-guided method.}
At the same budget \(N\), estimate reusable cells:
\[
    \hat \ell(a,s),
    \qquad
    \hat q(s\mid Z),
    \qquad
    \hat C_a(s,H),
    \qquad
    \hat F_a(s,H),
    \qquad
    \hat O_a^\delta(s,H).
\]
Then analytically score:
\[
    \hat J_{\mathrm{acct}}(d)
\]
for many \(d\in\mathcal D\), choose top finalists, and optionally confirm:
\[
    \hat d_N^{\mathrm{acct}}
    =
    \argmin_{d\in\mathrm{finalists}}\hat J_{\mathrm{confirm}}(d).
\]

\paragraph{Selection regret.}
For each method and budget \(N\), report:
\[
    \mathcal R_{\mathrm{sel}}(N)
    =
    J_{\mathrm{ref}}(\hat d_N)
    -
    \min_{d\in\mathcal D}J_{\mathrm{ref}}(d).
\]
Main plot:
\[
    N\mapsto \mathcal R_{\mathrm{sel}}(N)
\]
for black-box racing versus decomposition-guided selection.

\paragraph{Experiment I pass condition.}
Promote Message 5 if:
\[
    \mathcal R_{\mathrm{acct}}(N)
    <
    \mathcal R_{\mathrm{race}}(N)
\]
over the practically relevant budget range.
A strong headline is:
\[
    \textit{accounting reaches the same selected design as racing using fewer trajectories.}
\]

\paragraph{Experiment I ablations.}
Run:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
    \text{full accounting} & \text{reuse model-mode losses and signal posteriors},\\
    \text{no mode reuse} & \text{estimate each design separately by mode},\\
    \text{no signal reuse} & \text{refit router for each design},\\
    \text{no cost reuse} & \text{remeasure costs separately},\\
    \text{no confirmation} & \text{select analytically without finalist evaluation}.
\end{array}
\]
This identifies where the evaluation savings come from.

\paragraph{Experiment J: residual calibration.}
Goal:
\[
    \text{test whether the accounting prediction actually predicts held-out deployment loss.}
\]
For each design:
\[
    \hat J_{\mathrm{acct}}(d)
\]
from pilot components, and:
\[
    \hat J_{\mathrm{obs}}(d)
\]
from held-out end-to-end evaluation.
Plot:
\[
    \hat J_{\mathrm{acct}}(d)
    \quad\text{versus}\quad
    \hat J_{\mathrm{obs}}(d).
\]
Report:
\[
    R^2,
    \qquad
    \text{rank correlation},
    \qquad
    \max_{d\in\mathrm{finalists}}|\hat J_{\mathrm{acct}}(d)-\hat J_{\mathrm{obs}}(d)|.
\]

\paragraph{Residual diagnostics.}
Plot residuals by:
\[
    \text{mode},
    \qquad
    \text{model},
    \qquad
    \text{horizon},
    \qquad
    \text{router},
    \qquad
    \text{cost convention}.
\]
If residuals concentrate in one mode, the mode taxonomy is missing structure.
If residuals grow with horizon, the memoryless first-passage surrogate is insufficient.
If residuals concentrate by router, calibration or selection is the problem.

\paragraph{Memoryless surrogate check.}
For each \(a,s\), compare:
\[
    \hat F_a(s,h)
\]
to the geometric approximation:
\[
    1-(1-\hat p_a(s))^h.
\]
Report:
\[
    \max_{h\le H}
    \left|
    \hat F_a(s,h)-\{1-(1-\hat p_a(s))^h\}
    \right|.
\]
If this error is large, do not overstate the old memoryless theorem.
Use survival curves and occupancy as the operational losses.

\paragraph{Claim gates.}
Do not promote a claim unless its gate passes:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.27\linewidth}L{0.66\linewidth}@{}}
    \textbf{Certified effort claim} &
    $\mathrm{Reg}_{\mathrm{pass}}(\ell)>0$ or nontrivial rank inversions exist.\\
    \textbf{Mode-dependence claim} &
    $\mathrm{CA}_\ell>0$ and true modes beat shuffled modes.\\
    \textbf{Cheap-signal claim} &
    $\mathrm{VOI}_\ell(Z_{\mathrm{probe}})>0$ and beats budget/corrupt controls.\\
    \textbf{Router-quality claim} &
    $J(\hat r;Z)<J(a^\star_{\emptyset})$ and regret is small relative to opportunity.\\
    \textbf{Information-diagnostic claim} &
    I versus VOI or KL versus regret shows meaningful decoupling.\\
    \textbf{Cheap-evaluation claim} &
    $\mathcal R_{\mathrm{acct}}(N)<\mathcal R_{\mathrm{race}}(N)$ for relevant $N$.\\
    \textbf{Robustness claim} &
    Selected design is stable under bootstrap, threshold, cost, and mode-taxonomy changes.
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Potential negative results and how to write them.}
Negative results are useful if interpreted through the accounting.
If:
\[
    \mathrm{CA}_\ell\approx 0,
\]
write:
\begin{quote}\itshape
The current action menu has no exploitable mode-specific comparative advantage;
use the best single action.
\end{quote}
If:
\[
    \mathrm{CA}_\ell>0
    \quad\text{but}\quad
    \mathrm{VOI}_\ell(Z)\approx 0,
\]
write:
\begin{quote}\itshape
Different modes want different actions, but the deployable probe does not reveal
the modes well enough; improve the diagnostic signal.
\end{quote}
If:
\[
    \mathrm{VOI}_\ell(Z)>0
    \quad\text{but}\quad
    \mathrm{Reg}_\ell(\hat r)\text{ is large},
\]
write:
\begin{quote}\itshape
The signal is valuable, but the learned router fails to capture the available value;
improve calibration or the router class.
\end{quote}
If:
\[
    \mathcal R_{\mathrm{acct}}(N)>\mathcal R_{\mathrm{race}}(N),
\]
write:
\begin{quote}\itshape
The packed-mode decomposition is not sufficiently predictive for this design menu;
residuals identify the missing interaction.
\end{quote}
